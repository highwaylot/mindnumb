# Glossary

Plain definitions. Where a term has a specific Florida statutory meaning, the section is noted.

**Accord and satisfaction.** An agreement to accept something different from what was owed, plus performance of it, which discharges the original obligation. Cashing a check marked "payment in full" can do this (§ 673.3111).

**Affirmative defense.** A defense the defendant must plead and prove, which defeats the claim even if the plaintiff proves every element. Statute of limitations, waiver, release, estoppel.

**Agent.** A person authorized to act on behalf of another (the principal) in dealings with third parties.

**American rule.** Each party pays its own attorney fees unless a statute or contract says otherwise.

**Apparent authority.** Authority an agent appears to have because of the principal's words or conduct toward the third party, even if the principal never actually granted it.

**Articles of organization.** The document filed with the Florida Department of State to create an LLC (§ 605.0201).

**At-will employment.** Employment that either side may end at any time for any lawful reason or no reason. Florida's default.

**Binding authority.** Law a court must follow. See Module 00, Lesson 1.

**Business judgment rule.** The presumption that directors or managers who act on an informed basis, in good faith, and without conflict made a sound decision; courts will not second-guess it.

**Charging order.** A court order giving a judgment creditor the right to receive distributions a debtor-member would have received from an LLC, without making the creditor a member (§ 605.0503).

**Choice of law.** Which jurisdiction's law governs a dispute.

**Common law.** Law made by courts through decisions, as opposed to statutes.

**Comparative fault.** Reducing a plaintiff's recovery by the plaintiff's share of fault. Florida bars recovery if the plaintiff is more than 50% at fault (§ 768.81).

**Compensatory damages.** Money to make the injured party whole.

**Consequential damages.** Losses that flow from the breach but are not the direct value of the thing promised, such as lost profits. Recoverable only if foreseeable at contracting (*Hadley v. Baxendale*).

**Consideration.** Something of legal value exchanged for a promise. Required for a contract to be enforceable.

**Counterclaim.** A claim the defendant brings back against the plaintiff in the same case.

**De novo.** Review with no deference to the lower court. Used for questions of law.

**Dicta.** Statements in an opinion not necessary to the decision. Not binding.

**Disregarded entity.** A single-member LLC that the IRS ignores for income tax; the owner reports its income directly.

**Dissolution.** The start of the process of ending an entity. Followed by winding up and then termination.

**Distribution.** A transfer of money or property from an LLC to a member on account of the member's interest (§ 605.0102).

**Diversity jurisdiction.** Federal court authority over cases between citizens of different states where more than $75,000 is at stake.

**Duty of care.** The obligation of a manager, member, or director to act with the care a reasonably prudent person would use. In Florida LLCs, limited to refraining from grossly negligent or reckless conduct, willful misconduct, or knowing violation of law (§ 605.04091).

**Duty of loyalty.** The obligation not to compete with the entity, not to take its opportunities, and not to deal with it on unfair terms, without disclosure and consent.

**Economic loss rule.** A doctrine limiting tort recovery for purely financial loss. In Florida, now confined to products liability cases (*Tiara Condominium*).

**Element.** One of the required parts of a claim. All must be proven.

**En banc.** The full appellate court sitting together, rather than a panel.

**Equity.** The body of remedies and principles (injunctions, specific performance, estoppel, unclean hands) applied when money damages are inadequate or strict law would be unjust.

**Estoppel.** Being prevented from asserting something because of your own prior words or conduct that another relied on.

**FDUTPA.** The Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act, §§ 501.201–501.213.

**Fiduciary.** A person who owes duties of loyalty and care to another because of a relationship of trust: a manager to an LLC, an agent to a principal, a partner to a partnership.

**Forum selection clause.** A contract term fixing where disputes must be brought.

**Garden leave.** A period during which a departing employee remains employed and paid but does not work and cannot work for anyone else. Recognized in Florida's CHOICE Act.

**Holding.** The rule of law a case decides, limited to its facts.

**Indemnification.** One party's agreement to cover another's losses or liabilities.

**Injunction.** A court order to do or stop doing something. Preliminary (before trial) or permanent.

**Judgment creditor / judgment debtor.** The winner and loser of a money judgment, in their post-judgment roles.

**Jurisdiction.** A court's power to hear a case: over the subject matter and over the parties (personal jurisdiction).

**Laches.** An equitable defense that the plaintiff waited unreasonably long and the delay prejudiced the defendant.

**Legitimate business interest.** Under § 542.335, the interest an employer must prove to enforce a restrictive covenant: trade secrets, confidential information, substantial relationships with specific customers, goodwill, or specialized training.

**Liquidated damages.** A sum fixed in the contract as the damages for breach. Enforceable if a reasonable estimate and actual damages were hard to measure; unenforceable as a penalty otherwise.

**Manager-managed.** An LLC whose articles or operating agreement vest management in one or more managers rather than the members.

**Member.** An owner of an LLC.

**Member-managed.** An LLC run by its members directly. Florida's default.

**Mitigation.** The injured party's obligation to take reasonable steps to limit its losses.

**Operating agreement.** The agreement among members governing the LLC. May be written, oral, or implied (§ 605.0102(45)).

**Parol evidence rule.** Evidence of prior or contemporaneous agreements cannot be used to contradict a final written contract.

**Per curiam.** "By the court"; an unsigned opinion.

**Persuasive authority.** Law a court may consider but need not follow.

**Piercing the corporate veil.** Holding owners personally liable for an entity's debts, disregarding limited liability. Requires improper conduct in Florida (*Dania Jai-Alai*).

**Preemption.** Federal law displacing state law under the Supremacy Clause.

**Preponderance of the evidence.** More likely than not. The civil burden of proof.

**Prima facie.** On its face; enough evidence to require the other side to respond.

**Promissory estoppel.** Enforcing a promise without consideration because the promisee reasonably relied on it to their detriment.

**Proposal for settlement.** A formal offer under § 768.79 that shifts attorney fees if rejected and the judgment misses the offer by 25% or more.

**Punitive damages.** Damages to punish, beyond compensation. Require clear and convincing evidence of intentional misconduct or gross negligence in Florida (§ 768.72).

**Registered agent.** The person or company designated to receive legal papers for an entity. Required for every Florida LLC and corporation.

**Remand.** Sending a case back to the lower court.

**Respondeat superior.** The employer answers for torts an employee commits within the scope of employment.

**Restatement.** A treatise summarizing the common law in an area, written by the American Law Institute. Persuasive unless adopted by a court.

**Restrictive covenant.** A contract term restricting competition, solicitation, or disclosure. Governed by § 542.335 in Florida.

**Specific performance.** An order to perform the contract rather than pay damages. Available when the subject is unique, such as land.

**Standard of review.** The degree of deference an appellate court gives a trial court's ruling.

**Stare decisis.** The principle that courts follow precedent.

**Statute of frauds.** The rule that certain contracts must be in a signed writing: land, contracts not performable within a year, guaranties, goods of $500 or more (§ 725.01; § 672.201).

**Statute of limitations.** The deadline for filing a claim (§ 95.11).

**Statute of repose.** An absolute outer deadline that runs from an event (sale, completion) regardless of when harm was discovered (§ 95.031).

**Summary judgment.** Judgment without trial because there is no genuine dispute of material fact. Florida adopted the federal standard in 2021 (Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.510).

**Tortious interference.** Intentionally and unjustifiably disrupting another's contract or business relationship.

**Transferable interest.** A member's right to receive distributions, which can be assigned; the assignee does not become a member (§ 605.0501).

**Unconscionability.** A defense to enforcement where a contract term is grossly unfair and was imposed through an unfair process.

**Unjust enrichment.** A claim to recover a benefit conferred on another when it would be unjust for them to keep it without paying.

**Venue.** The county or district where a case should be heard.

**Vicarious liability.** Liability for another's conduct because of a relationship, not because of one's own fault.

**Waiver.** Intentionally giving up a known right.

**Winding up.** Settling an entity's affairs after dissolution: collecting assets, paying creditors, distributing what remains.
