# Florida taxes for business

**Module:** 07 · **Lesson:** 2 · **Time:** ~50 min
**Tags:** `[US]` `[FL]`

## Why this matters to a business owner

Florida's low-tax reputation is about personal income tax. Businesses still collect sales tax as an agent of the state, pay reemployment tax on wages, file a tangible property return, and pay federal taxes that dwarf everything else. Sales tax is where small businesses get hurt, because it is the state's money held in trust and the owner can be personally liable for it.

## The rule

**No personal income tax.** Fla. Const. art. VII, § 5 bars it. Income that passes through to an individual owner is not taxed by Florida.

**Corporate income tax (ch. 220).** C corporations, and LLCs that elect C corporation treatment, pay 5.5% (verify) on Florida-apportioned taxable income, with the first $50,000 exempt (verify) (§ 220.14). S corporations and LLCs taxed as partnerships or disregarded entities generally owe nothing and, for S corporations, usually need not file unless they owe federal tax (verify).

**Sales and use tax (ch. 212).** The state rate is 6% (verify) on retail sales of tangible personal property, plus a county discretionary surtax of 0 to 2% (verify) (§ 212.055). Also taxable: admissions, transient rentals under six months, commercial cleaning, pest control, detective and security services, and some others listed in § 212.05. Most services are not taxable. The dealer collects from the customer and remits. **Use tax** is the mirror: if you buy taxable goods for use in Florida and no sales tax was collected, you owe the same rate directly.

- **Registration.** Before making taxable sales, register with the Department of Revenue (§ 212.18).
- **Resale.** A registered dealer buying for resale gives the seller its Annual Resale Certificate and the purchase is exempt.
- **Commercial rent.** Florida was the only state that taxed commercial rent. That tax under § 212.031 was repealed effective October 1, 2025 (verify).
- **Nexus and remote sellers.** A seller with no Florida presence must collect once it exceeds $100,000 (verify) in Florida remote sales in the prior calendar year (§ 212.0596). Marketplace providers collect for their sellers (§ 212.05965).

**Reemployment tax (ch. 443).** The employer pays on the first $7,000 (verify) of each employee's annual wages. New employers start at 2.7% (verify); the rate then moves with the employer's claims history.

**Tangible personal property tax (ch. 193 and § 196.183).** Business furniture, fixtures, and equipment are taxed by the county property appraiser. A return (Form DR-405) is due April 1 (verify). The first $25,000 (verify) of value is exempt, and once a timely return shows value under that amount, the appraiser may waive future returns (§ 196.183) (verify).

**Documentary stamp tax (ch. 201).** On promissory notes and other written obligations to pay, 35 cents per $100 (verify), capped at $2,450 (verify) for unsecured notes (§ 201.08). On deeds, 70 cents per $100 (verify) of consideration (§ 201.02); Miami-Dade differs.

**Federal taxes, in outline.**

- **Self-employment tax.** Sole proprietors and partners, including most LLC members, pay 15.3% (verify) on net earnings up to the Social Security wage base, and 2.9% (verify) above it, in addition to income tax.
- **S corporation election.** An LLC can elect S status (Form 2553). Owner-employees must then be paid "reasonable compensation" as wages subject to payroll tax; remaining profit passes through free of self-employment tax.
- **Estimated taxes.** Owners of pass-through entities pay quarterly estimates (Form 1040-ES).
- **Information returns.** Form 1099-NEC for nonemployee compensation. The threshold was $600 through 2025 and rises to $2,000 (verify) for payments made after December 31, 2025, under the 2025 federal tax act.

**Burden.** In a Department of Revenue assessment, the assessment is presumed correct and the taxpayer must prove it wrong. Exemptions are construed against the taxpayer.

## How courts reason about it

Tax disputes are statutory construction with a thumb on the scale.

- **Taxable or not.** Courts read taxing statutes strictly against the state when deciding whether something is taxed at all, and read exemptions strictly against the taxpayer. So the state must show the transaction falls inside the words of § 212.05; the dealer must show it falls inside an exemption in § 212.08. Mixed transactions, where a service comes with goods, turn on whether the goods are the real object of the deal or incidental to the service.
- **Trust fund liability.** Sales tax collected is state money. Section 213.29 makes any person required to collect it who willfully fails to remit personally liable for a penalty of twice the tax (verify). Courts apply this to officers, managers, and members who had check-signing authority and knew taxes were unpaid. The LLC shield does not help because the liability is personal under the statute. Federal law has the same rule for withheld payroll taxes, 26 U.S.C. § 6672, and courts hold responsible persons liable even where they paid other creditors to keep the business alive.
- **Classification.** Reemployment tax and payroll tax follow the employee-contractor line (module 05). Courts use the common-law control test.
- **Procedure.** A Florida tax assessment is challenged by protest, then either an administrative hearing under ch. 120 or a suit in circuit court under § 72.011. The suit generally requires paying the uncontested amount first. Missing the 60-day (verify) window to contest makes the assessment final.

## Florida specifics

- Florida follows the majority of states on sales tax design: a destination-based tax on tangible goods with enumerated services. It departs by taxing commercial rent, which ended October 1, 2025.
- The discretionary surtax applies only to the first $5,000 (verify) of a single sale of tangible personal property (§ 212.054(2)(b)).
- Florida's no-income-tax rule means the choice between S corporation and partnership taxation is driven entirely by federal self-employment tax and by Florida's corporate tax if C status is elected.

## Angles checklist

- Is each product or service taxable under § 212.05, exempt under § 212.08, or outside the statute? Who says so, and in writing?
- Is the business registered as a dealer, and filing on the correct frequency?
- Resale certificates on file for exempt sales, and verified?
- Out-of-state sales: have thresholds in other states been crossed?
- Commercial lease: did the landlord stop charging sales tax on rent for periods after October 1, 2025?
- Payroll: reemployment tax registered, workers classified, payroll deposits current? Who signs the checks?
- Tangible property return filed by April 1?
- Promissory notes: stamp tax paid before suing on the note?
- Federal: entity classification elected, reasonable compensation documented, estimates paid, W-9s collected, 1099s issued?
- Personal exposure: who is a "responsible person" for unpaid trust fund taxes?

## Drill

Maya's single-member LLC sells custom furniture from a Sarasota showroom and ships nationally. She also charges a design fee for in-home consultations. The showroom lease includes "rent plus sales tax." She pays her two assemblers as 1099 contractors and signs all checks. Cash is tight, and she has skipped three sales tax filings to make payroll. What are the tax issues?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- **Furniture sales.** Tangible personal property, taxable at 6% plus Sarasota County surtax on the first $5,000 of each item.
- **Design fee.** A service, generally not taxable, unless bundled with the furniture such that the furniture is the object of the transaction.
- **Lease.** Sales tax on commercial rent ended October 1, 2025. If the landlord is still charging it for later periods, Maya is overpaying and can demand a correction; the landlord cannot keep tax it was not required to collect.
- **Assemblers.** Workers assembling furniture at her direction, on her premises, with her tools, look like employees under the control test. Reclassification means reemployment tax, federal payroll tax, and possibly workers' compensation if the count reaches four.
- **Skipped filings.** The tax she collected is state money. She is personally liable under § 213.29, and the Department can revoke her dealer registration, file a tax warrant, and freeze accounts. The LLC does not protect her.
- **Federal.** As a disregarded entity, she pays self-employment tax on net profit and should be paying quarterly estimates. An S election might reduce self-employment tax if profits are large enough to justify payroll costs.

</details>

## Check yourself

1. A Florida LLC taxed as a partnership earns $400,000. What Florida income tax does it or its members owe?
2. A customer presents a resale certificate and buys $10,000 of goods, then uses them in her own office. Who owes the tax?
3. Why can the state collect unpaid sales tax from an LLC's manager personally without piercing the veil?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. None. Florida has no personal income tax and the LLC is not a C corporation. Federal income and self-employment tax still apply.
2. The customer, as use tax, plus penalties for misusing the certificate. The seller who accepted a facially valid certificate in good faith is generally protected (verify).
3. Because § 213.29 imposes liability directly on any person required to collect the tax who willfully fails to remit. That is a statutory duty of the individual, not an entity debt. Veil piercing is about reaching owners for entity debts; this is not one.

</details>
