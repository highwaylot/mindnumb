# Drill: The handshake partner

**Modules exercised:** [02 Business entities](../02-business-entities/03-operating-agreements.md), [03 Contracts](../03-contracts/02-consideration-and-promissory-estoppel.md), [08 Disputes](../08-disputes-procedure/01-life-of-a-lawsuit.md)
**Time:** ~60 min

## Scenario

In January 2025, Dana and Marcus decide to open a mobile boat-detailing business in St. Petersburg. Dana files Articles of Organization on Sunbiz for "Tidewater Detail LLC." She is the only person named on the filing, as the authorized person and the sole manager. She tells Marcus she did it that way "to keep it simple" and that they are "50/50, obviously."

Marcus wires $40,000 to the new LLC's bank account for a trailer, a pressure washer, and supplies. Dana puts in $5,000. For the next eighteen months Marcus does most of the physical work, six days a week, and takes no salary. Dana handles bookings, invoicing, and the books. Both are on the LLC's bank account as signers. The 2025 tax return was prepared by Dana's cousin as a single-member LLC on Dana's Schedule C.

By mid-2026 the business is clearing about $12,000 a month. Dana pays herself $6,000 a month as a "management fee." She has paid Marcus a total of $14,000 in irregular transfers labeled "draw." In September 2026 Marcus asks for a written operating agreement and his half of the profits. Dana replies by text: "You were never a member. You loaned the company money and you worked here. I'll pay back the $40k when cash allows."

Marcus wants out, with his money and his share. Today is October 29, 2026.

## Your task

List every legal issue you can see. For each: who could claim what against whom, what rule governs, what the likely outcome is, and what facts you would want to know that the scenario does not give you.

Write your answer before opening the model answer.

<summary>Model answer</summary>

**Step 1, parties.** Marcus. Dana. Tidewater Detail LLC, a separate legal person whose owner is the thing in dispute. Dana's cousin, the preparer, is a witness, not a party.

**Step 2, timeline.** January 2025, LLC filed, oral 50/50 statement. Shortly after, $40,000 contribution. 2025 through 2026, labor and partial draws. Early 2026, Schedule C return treats Dana as sole owner. September 2026, the text that denies membership. The denial is the breach date for most claims; nothing is time-barred.

**Steps 3 and 4.** The issues, with each side.

### Issue 1: Is Marcus a member?
- **Parties and claim:** Marcus against Dana and the LLC, for a declaration that he is a 50% member.
- **Rule:** The Sunbiz filing does not list members and does not decide ownership. After formation, a person becomes a member as provided in the operating agreement or with the consent of all members (§ 605.0401, Fla. Stat.). An operating agreement may be oral, implied, in a record, or any combination (§ 605.0102(45)). Marcus bears the burden of proving the oral agreement by a preponderance.
- **Application:** "50/50, obviously," a $40,000 contribution, eighteen months of unpaid labor, signature authority on the account, and transfers labeled "draw" are strong evidence of an oral operating agreement making him a member. The Schedule C and the "management fee" cut the other way, and both were in Dana's control.
- **Likely outcome:** More likely than not a court finds an oral agreement that Marcus is a member. Courts weigh conduct over labels, and "draw" is an owner's word.
- **Facts that would change it:** Texts from January 2025. Whether Marcus ever signed anything calling himself an employee or lender. Whether the $40,000 was ever documented as a loan with interest or a repayment date. What Dana told the bank and the insurer.

### Issue 2: If a member, what is he owed?
- **Parties and claim:** Marcus against the LLC for distributions and against Dana for breach of the operating agreement.
- **Rule:** Absent agreement, distributions before dissolution are shared on the basis of the agreed value of each member's contributions as stated in the company's records (§ 605.0404(1)), and votes are in proportion to profits interests (§ 605.04073). An oral 50/50 agreement displaces those defaults. A member has no right to a salary for services by default; a "management fee" to one member is a distribution or a conflicted transaction unless agreed.
- **Application:** If 50/50 was agreed, the $6,000 monthly fee to Dana was an unequal distribution. If nothing was agreed on shares, the statutory default would favor Marcus even more, since his contribution was $40,000 against $5,000.
- **Likely outcome:** An accounting, with Dana's fees recharacterized and Marcus credited for the shortfall.
- **Facts that would change it:** Whether they ever discussed pay for Dana's office work. The company's actual books.

### Issue 3: Fiduciary duty and information rights
- **Parties and claim:** Marcus against Dana for breach of the duties of loyalty and care; Marcus against the LLC to inspect records.
- **Rule:** In a manager-managed LLC the manager owes the company and the members the duties of loyalty and care set out in § 605.04091, including accounting for profits and refraining from self-dealing without disclosure and consent. Members have the right to inspect records (§ 605.0410). A member may sue directly for injury to the member or derivatively for injury to the company (§§ 605.0801, 605.0802).
- **Application:** Paying herself a fee she never disclosed as such and filing a return that erases Marcus are loyalty questions. The claim for his own distributions is direct. A claim to recover the fees for the company is derivative and requires a demand or an excuse (§ 605.0802).
- **Likely outcome:** Viable if membership is proven. Dead if it is not, because a non-member is owed no fiduciary duty.
- **Facts that would change it:** Whether Dana disclosed the fee and Marcus went along. Whether the fee is market rate for the work.

### Issue 4: If not a member, what then?
- **Parties and claim:** Marcus against the LLC and Dana in unjust enrichment, quantum meruit, promissory estoppel, and on the loan Dana now admits.
- **Rule:** Unjust enrichment requires a benefit conferred, knowingly accepted, that would be inequitable to retain without paying. Quantum meruit values services. Promissory estoppel enforces a clear promise reasonably relied on to the promisee's detriment; Florida recognizes it but requires the promise to be proven by clear and convincing evidence (*W.R. Grace & Co. v. Geodata Services, Inc.*, 547 So. 2d 919 (Fla. 1989)). An admitted loan is a debt. See [`cases/hoffman-v-red-owl.md`](../cases/hoffman-v-red-owl.md).
- **Application:** These are pleaded in the alternative. Dana's own text concedes $40,000 is owed. Eighteen months of labor at $14,000 total is far under minimum wage, which opens a different door: if Dana insists he was a worker, he was an underpaid employee with a Florida minimum wage claim (§ 448.110) and possibly an FLSA claim. Dana cannot have it both ways.
- **Likely outcome:** At minimum, $40,000 plus interest, and a reasonable value for the labor net of the $14,000 paid.
- **Facts that would change it:** Hours worked. Whether any part of the $14,000 was called wages. Whether the statute of frauds was raised; an agreement of indefinite duration is performable within a year, so § 725.01 does not bar it.

### Issue 5: How does Marcus get out?
- **Parties and claim:** Marcus against the LLC and Dana for a buyout or dissolution.
- **Rule:** A member may dissociate at any time by express will (§ 605.0601). But in a Florida LLC, unlike a partnership, dissociation does not trigger a buyout. The dissociated member keeps only a transferable interest, the right to distributions if and when made (§§ 605.0603, 605.0502). To force value out, Marcus must either negotiate, or petition for judicial dissolution on grounds such as that the managers are acting fraudulently or that it is not reasonably practicable to carry on the company in conformity with the operating agreement (§ 605.0702). The company or the other members may then elect to buy his interest at fair value instead of dissolving (§ 605.0706).
- **Application:** Walking away gets him nothing but an IOU. A dissolution petition is the lever that produces a fair-value buyout.
- **Likely outcome:** Settlement at a negotiated value once the petition is filed.
- **Facts that would change it:** What the business is worth. Whether Dana has the cash to buy.

**Step 6, what you do not know.** The exact words in January 2025. Who has the books. What the insurance application said about ownership. Whether Marcus signed any W-9, 1099, or loan paper. Whether the 2025 return can be amended to a partnership return and what that costs.

### Red herrings
- "Her name is on Sunbiz." The Articles name an authorized person and a manager. They do not list owners and do not create ownership.
- "There is no written operating agreement, so there is no agreement." § 605.0102(45) says otherwise.
- "The statute of frauds kills an oral deal." Not one performable within a year.
- Veil piercing. Nobody is trying to reach a member for the LLC's debts.

### Procedural and remedial angles
- Forum: circuit court in Pinellas County (Sixth Judicial Circuit). The claims exceed $50,000 (verify) and dissolution and accounting are equitable. The LLC must appear through counsel.
- Value: $40,000 plus accrued distributions plus half the going-concern value, or alternatively $40,000 plus quantum meruit.
- Fees: American rule. No fee clause, no fee statute, unless the wage theory is used (§ 448.110 and the FLSA shift fees to a prevailing worker).
- Limitations: oral contract 4 years (verify); breach of fiduciary duty and unjust enrichment 4 years (verify) (§ 95.11). All run from September 2026 at the latest.
- Pre-suit: a records demand under § 605.0410 is cheap and forces Dana to produce the books. A demand letter proposing a buyout and mediation comes next.
