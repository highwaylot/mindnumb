# Mediation and arbitration

**Module:** 08 · **Lesson:** 5 · **Time:** ~50 min
**Tags:** `[FL]` `[US]`

## Why this matters to a business owner

Nearly every Florida civil case goes to mediation before trial, and many business contracts route disputes to arbitration before any court sees them. Mediation is a structured negotiation with no decision-maker. Arbitration is a private trial with almost no appeal. Choosing arbitration in a contract is a decision made years before any dispute, and it changes cost, speed, confidentiality, discovery, and the chance of correcting a bad result.

## The rule

### Mediation

**Court-ordered mediation.** Under § 44.102 and Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.700, a court may refer any contested civil matter to mediation, and most Florida circuits do so by standing order. A party, or a representative with full authority to settle without consulting anyone else, must attend, along with counsel (Rule 1.720(b)). Failure to appear with authority leads to sanctions including fees and costs (Rule 1.720(f)).

**Confidentiality.** The Mediation Confidentiality and Privilege Act, §§ 44.401 to 44.406, makes mediation communications confidential and privileged. They cannot be disclosed or used in court. Exceptions in § 44.405(4): the signed written agreement itself, communications that are themselves a crime or used to plan one, threats of violence, and professional misconduct, among others. Breach of confidentiality carries fees, costs, and damages.

**Outcome.** If the parties agree, the mediator reduces it to a written agreement signed by the parties and counsel (Rule 1.730(b)). It is a contract, enforceable by motion in the same case. If not, the mediator reports only "impasse" to the court.

### Arbitration

**Sources of law.** The Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C. §§ 1 to 16, applies to any arbitration clause in a contract "evidencing a transaction involving commerce,". The Revised Florida Arbitration Code, ch. 682, governs the rest and fills procedural gaps. Where they conflict, the FAA controls, and the FAA preempts any state rule that disfavors arbitration (*AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion*, 563 U.S. 333 (2011)).

**Enforcement.** A valid arbitration clause is enforced like any contract. A party sued in court moves to compel arbitration and stay the case (9 U.S.C. §§ 3, 4; § 682.03). The court decides three things (*Seifert v. U.S. Home Corp.*, 750 So. 2d 633 (Fla. 1999)): (1) is there a valid written agreement to arbitrate, (2) does the dispute fall within it, and (3) has the right been waived. Florida reads scope by whether the claim has a "significant relationship" to the contract. If the clause delegates arbitrability to the arbitrator in clear terms, the court sends even those questions to the arbitrator (*Rent-A-Center, West, Inc. v. Jackson*, 561 U.S. 63 (2010)).

**Waiver.** A party that litigates in court, takes discovery, and then moves to compel has waived. No showing of prejudice is required under the FAA (*Morgan v. Sundance, Inc.*, 596 U.S. 411 (2022)). Move at the first opportunity or lose it.

**Class waivers.** A clause requiring individual arbitration and waiving class actions is enforceable in consumer contracts (*Concepcion*) and employment contracts (*Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis*, 584 U.S. 497 (2018)).

**Review.** An award can be vacated only on narrow grounds: corruption, evident partiality, arbitrator misconduct, or the arbitrator exceeding powers (9 U.S.C. § 10; § 682.13). Legal error is not a ground. Parties cannot contract for broader judicial review under the FAA (*Hall Street Associates, L.L.C. v. Mattel, Inc.*, 552 U.S. 576 (2008)). The award is confirmed and becomes a court judgment (§ 682.12).

**Providers and cost.** The American Arbitration Association and JAMS are the common administrators. Filing fees scale with the claim and run into the thousands; the arbitrator bills hourly, often $400 to $900 (verify). Provider rules for consumer and employment cases shift most of those costs to the business. A three-arbitrator panel triples the cost.

**Burden.** The party seeking to compel proves an agreement; the party resisting proves a contract defense such as unconscionability, or waiver. The party seeking vacatur bears a heavy burden.

## How courts reason about it

- **Formation defenses only.** Courts decide whether an arbitration agreement was formed and whether it is valid under general contract law. Defenses that apply to contracts generally, like fraud in the inducement of the clause itself, duress, or unconscionability, are heard. Defenses aimed at arbitration as such are preempted.
- **Online and form contracts.** Whether a clause in website terms binds a user is a formation question decided under *Nguyen* and *ProCD* principles: was there reasonable notice and manifestation of assent. Clickwrap usually binds; browsewrap usually does not.
- **Non-signatories.** Courts let a non-signatory enforce or be bound by a clause through agency, estoppel, or third-party beneficiary theories when the claims are intertwined with the contract. An LLC's owner sued personally may be able to invoke the LLC's clause.
- **Scope.** Broad clauses ("any dispute arising out of or relating to") reach tort and statutory claims connected to the relationship. Narrow clauses ("disputes under this agreement") reach contract claims only. Florida's significant relationship test asks whether the claim requires reference to the contract.
- **Mediation enforcement.** A mediated settlement agreement is enforced as a contract. Courts refuse to look behind it absent fraud, and the confidentiality statute keeps the negotiations out. Agreements that leave material terms open sometimes fail for indefiniteness; a complete signed agreement at the table is the goal.
- **Sanctions for empty chairs.** Courts sanction parties who send a representative without real authority, or who attend by phone without leave. The point of Rule 1.720 is that the person who can say yes is in the room.

## Florida specifics

- Florida follows the majority of states in adopting the Revised Uniform Arbitration Act (ch. 682, effective 2013) and in enforcing arbitration clauses under the FAA.
- Florida's near-universal court-ordered mediation is a stronger practice than in many states, and its confidentiality statute is detailed and enforced.
- Florida also allows court-ordered nonbinding arbitration under § 44.103, where a party who rejects the award and does worse at trial can be assessed costs and fees (verify).
- A Florida court may order mediation even where the contract requires arbitration, if the parties are already in court on a non-arbitrable claim.

## Angles checklist

- Is there an arbitration clause anywhere in the relationship: the contract, the terms of service, the purchase order, the employee handbook?
- Scope: broad or narrow? Does it reach tort and statutory claims? Does it bind the individual owner?
- Delegation clause: who decides arbitrability?
- Carve-outs: injunctive relief in court, small claims, lien rights?
- Rules and provider, seat, number of arbitrators, governing law, fee allocation, confidentiality, class waiver.
- Waiver risk: has anyone already litigated?
- Mediation: who has authority, what is the realistic range, what documents prove it, what would a complete signed agreement need to contain?
- Confidentiality: what can and cannot be used later from the mediation?
- Flip it: does the other side benefit more from arbitration's privacy and lack of appeal than you do?

## Drill

Tidewater Marine Supply signs a dealer agreement with a manufacturer. The agreement says "any dispute arising out of or relating to this agreement shall be resolved by binding arbitration under AAA Commercial Rules in Jacksonville." Tidewater believes the manufacturer defrauded it about exclusivity and sues in Duval circuit court for fraud and FDUTPA. The manufacturer answers, serves discovery, takes Tidewater's owner's deposition, and five months later moves to compel arbitration. The court orders mediation first. Spot the issues.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- **Agreement and scope.** A valid written clause exists. "Arising out of or relating to" is broad. Fraud and FDUTPA claims tied to the exclusivity promise have a significant relationship to the agreement and are within scope. Fraud in the inducement of the contract goes to the arbitrator; only fraud directed at the clause itself stays in court.
- **Waiver.** The manufacturer answered without raising arbitration, served discovery, and took a deposition. That is litigation conduct inconsistent with the right. Under *Morgan*, Tidewater need not show prejudice. The motion to compel is likely denied for waiver.
- **Mediation.** The court can order it regardless. Tidewater must send someone with full authority. Communications are privileged under § 44.405. If a deal is reached, it must be fully written and signed at the table.
- **Facts that would change it.** If the manufacturer had moved to compel in its first filing, arbitration would be ordered, the case stayed, and Tidewater would face AAA fees and limited discovery with no appeal on legal error. If the clause delegated arbitrability, even the scope fight would go to the arbitrator.

</details>

## Check yourself

1. An arbitrator misreads the statute of limitations and dismisses a clearly timely claim. Can a court fix it?
2. A party's lawyer attends mediation; the party joins by phone and says "I need to call my partner before I can agree to anything." Problem?
3. Why do Florida courts refuse to hear defenses that arbitration is unfair or too expensive compared to court?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Almost never. Legal error is not a ground for vacatur under 9 U.S.C. § 10 or § 682.13. Unless the arbitrator exceeded the powers granted by the agreement, the award stands.
2. Yes. Rule 1.720(b) requires a party representative with full authority to settle without further consultation, physically present unless the court or the other parties agree otherwise. Sanctions can follow.
3. Because the FAA preempts state rules that treat arbitration agreements less favorably than other contracts. Only generally applicable contract defenses survive, and *Concepcion* holds that even those cannot be used to defeat the fundamental attributes of arbitration.

</details>
