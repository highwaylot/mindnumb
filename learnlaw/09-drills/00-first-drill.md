# Drill: The handshake landscapers

**Modules exercised:** [00 How law works](../00-how-law-works/05-issue-spotting-method.md), [02 Business entities](../02-business-entities/08-partnerships-and-the-accidental-partnership.md), [03 Contracts](../03-contracts/01-formation.md), [06 Property and IP](../06-property-ip/02-trademarks.md), [08 Disputes](../08-disputes-procedure/02-small-claims-and-county-court.md)
**Time:** ~45 min

## Scenario

In March 2025, Marcus and Theo, friends since high school, start mowing lawns and installing sod around Orlando. They never sign anything. They agree over beers to split the profits 50/50. They call the business "GreenLine Lawn & Landscape," print it on shirts and yard signs, and open an Instagram account under that name. 
In June 2025, Marcus buys a used pickup for $14,000. The title is in his name alone. He paid $6,000 from savings and $8,000 from the business checking account, a personal account in Theo's name that both use.

In August 2026, GreenLine installs sod and an irrigation line for a homeowner in Winter Park. The invoice is $3,200. The homeowner refuses to pay. He says the sod died within three weeks and the irrigation line leaks. Theo says the homeowner never watered it.

Now, in October 2026, Marcus and Theo are fighting. Theo wants to keep running GreenLine without Marcus. Marcus says the name is his because he thought of it. Theo says the truck belongs to the business.

## Your task

List every legal issue you can see. For each: who could claim what against whom, what rule governs, what the likely outcome is, and what facts you would want to know that the scenario does not give you.

Write your answer before opening the model answer.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

Work the seven steps in order.

**Step 1, parties.** Marcus (individual). Theo (individual). GreenLine (an entity, if one exists, and that is the first question). The Winter Park homeowner (individual customer). Relationships: Marcus and Theo are co-owners or nothing; GreenLine and the homeowner had an oral services contract; Marcus holds title to a truck that may or may not be his.

**Step 2, timeline.** March 2025, business starts, oral 50/50 deal. June 2025, truck bought with mixed money, titled to Marcus. August 2026, sod job and refusal to pay. October 2026, the falling out. Today is October 2026.

**Steps 3 and 4, walk the doctrines and flip them.** The issues below are the result.

### Issue 1: Is GreenLine a partnership?
- **Parties and claim:** Marcus and Theo, each against the other, over whether they co-own a business.
- **Rule:** Two or more persons who carry on as co-owners a business for profit form a partnership, whether or not they intend to (§ 620.8202(1), Fla. Stat.). Sharing profits is presumed to make someone a partner unless the share was wages, rent, or loan interest (§ 620.8202(3)). No writing is needed.
- **Application:** They split profits 50/50, both work, both hold themselves out under one name. That is a general partnership by default.
- **Likely outcome:** Partnership. Each is a partner with an equal share of profits and losses (§ 620.8401) and each is jointly and severally liable for partnership debts (§ 620.8306).
- **Facts that would change it:** Any text, email, or tax return showing one paid the other wages instead of a profit share. Whether they filed a partnership return or each reported income as a sole proprietor.

### Issue 2: Who owns the truck?
- **Parties and claim:** Theo, for the partnership, against Marcus; or Marcus against the partnership for the $6,000.
- **Rule:** Property bought with partnership funds is presumed partnership property even if titled in a partner's name. Property bought in one partner's name without partnership funds is presumed that partner's separate property (§ 620.8204, Fla. Stat.).
- **Application:** $8,000 of $14,000 came from the shared account. The title alone does not decide it.
- **Likely outcome:** Probably partnership property, with Marcus entitled to credit for his $6,000 as a contribution or a loan.
- **Facts that would change it:** Whether the $8,000 was treated as a draw by Marcus. Whether the shared account was really the business account or Theo's personal money.

### Issue 3: The unpaid $3,200 invoice
- **Parties and claim:** GreenLine (the partnership) against the homeowner for breach of an oral contract.
- **Rule:** An oral services contract is enforceable. The limitation period for a contract not founded on a written instrument is 4 years (verify) (§ 95.11, Fla. Stat.). A partnership may sue in its own name (§ 620.8307). Claims up to $8,000 (verify) exclusive of interest, costs, and fees go to small claims in county court, where a business can appear through a partner or employee without a lawyer.
- **Application:** The job was done and invoiced. The homeowner's defense is defective work, which is a set-off or a counterclaim. If GreenLine sues under its fictitious name without registering it under § 865.09, the court can stay the action until it registers (§ 865.09(9), verify).
- **Likely outcome:** A small claims case in Orange County, likely settled at mediation, which the Small Claims Rules require at the pretrial conference. Recovery depends on proof that the sod was installed properly and the leak was not GreenLine's fault.
- **Facts that would change it:** Any written estimate or text that set terms or a warranty. Photos. Whether the homeowner watered. Whether a licensed irrigation contractor was required for the line.

### Issue 4: The homeowner's counterclaim for defective work
- **Parties and claim:** Homeowner against GreenLine, and against Marcus and Theo personally as partners.
- **Rule:** A service provider impliedly promises to perform in a workmanlike manner. Damages are the cost to repair or replace. A counterclaim arising from the same job is compulsory and must be brought in the same case (Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.170(a)). Partners are personally liable for partnership obligations (§ 620.8306).
- **Application:** Dead sod and a leaking line are the kind of facts that support a counterclaim.
- **Likely outcome:** A wash or a modest net recovery either way, decided on photographs and the homeowner's own conduct.
- **Facts that would change it:** The cause of the sod death. Whether GreenLine offered to fix the leak.

### Issue 5: Who owns the name "GreenLine Lawn & Landscape"?
- **Parties and claim:** Marcus against Theo, each claiming the name.
- **Rule:** Trademark rights in the United States come from use in commerce, not from thinking of the name or from a Sunbiz filing. A fictitious name registration under § 865.09 is a notice statute; the statute itself says it confers no rights in the name (verify subsection). Goodwill built under a partnership's name is partnership property (§ 620.8204).
- **Application:** Neither partner used the name alone. The partnership used it. The name and its goodwill belong to the partnership, not to Marcus because he thought of it, and not to Theo because he wants to keep going.
- **Likely outcome:** On a breakup, the name is an asset to be valued and allocated in winding up, or one partner buys the other's interest including the goodwill. A race to file a fictitious name or a ch. 495 registration will not change ownership.
- **Facts that would change it:** Whether someone else in Florida was using "GreenLine" first. Whether the Instagram account and domain are in one person's name.

### Issue 6: Ending the partnership
- **Parties and claim:** Either partner, to dissolve or to be bought out.
- **Rule:** A partnership at will dissolves when a partner gives notice of an express will to withdraw (§ 620.8801(1)). The partners then wind up: pay debts, including the homeowner if he wins, then split what is left (§ 620.8807). Alternatively, one partner can dissociate and be bought out at the value of his interest (§ 620.8701).
- **Application:** Theo cannot simply "keep GreenLine" and leave Marcus out. He can buy Marcus out, or they dissolve and split the truck, the receivables, and the name.
- **Likely outcome:** A negotiated buyout, with the truck and the name as the main items.
- **Facts that would change it:** Any text on what happens if one leaves.

**Step 6, what you do not know.** Who paid the truck insurance and tax. What the sod estimate said. Whether the shared account was ever in both names. Whether either of them has a local business tax receipt and whether the irrigation work needed a license (verify with Orange County and DBPR).

### Red herrings
- "We never signed anything, so there is no business." Wrong. Chapter 620 creates the partnership from conduct.
- "The truck is in my name, so it is mine." Title is evidence, not the answer. The source of funds controls.
- "I thought of the name." Idea without use creates no trademark right.
- "Register the name first and win." Fictitious name registration is a notice filing, not a deed.

### Procedural and remedial angles
- The invoice: small claims, Orange County court (Ninth Judicial Circuit), under $8,000 (verify). Filing fee in the low hundreds (verify). No attorney fees either way unless a statute or contract shifts them; none here. Interest at the statutory judgment rate. Four years from August 2026 to file (verify).
- The partnership dispute: an accounting and dissolution is an equitable claim in circuit court, expensive relative to the assets. Mediate first.
- Personal exposure: because this is a general partnership, both are personally liable for the homeowner's counterclaim and anything else the business owes. That is the single most important fact in the story, and the reason the next drill is about forming an LLC properly.
