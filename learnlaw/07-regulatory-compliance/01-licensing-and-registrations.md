# Licensing and registrations

**Module:** 07 · **Lesson:** 1 · **Time:** ~45 min
**Tags:** `[US]` `[FL]`

## Why this matters to a business owner

Forming an LLC on Sunbiz is step one of perhaps six. Most businesses also need a state license, a local tax receipt, a tax registration, and sometimes a zoning approval before the first sale is lawful. Missing the wrong one can make your contracts unenforceable and hand the other side a statutory claim with fee shifting. This lesson maps who requires what and why the consequences differ.

## The rule

There is no single licensing statute. There is a stack of regimes, each with its own trigger, agency, and penalty. Walk the stack every time.

**State occupational and business licenses.** Florida licenses by activity, not by entity type. The question is "what does the business do," not "what is it called."

- **Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR).** Construction contracting (ch. 489), real estate (ch. 475), cosmetology and barbering (chs. 476, 477), restaurants and lodging (ch. 509), alcoholic beverages (chs. 561 to 565), architecture, engineering, accountancy, and dozens of other trades.
- **Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS).** Commercial telephone sellers (§§ 501.601 to 501.626), sellers of travel (§ 559.927), health studios (§§ 501.012 to 501.019), motor vehicle repair shops (§§ 559.901 to 559.9221), charitable solicitation (ch. 496), pawnbrokers (ch. 539).
- **Department of Health (DOH).** Health professions under ch. 456 and the individual practice acts.
- **Office of Financial Regulation (OFR).** Lenders (ch. 516), mortgage brokers and lenders (ch. 494), money transmitters and check cashers (ch. 560), securities dealers and investment advisers (ch. 517).

**Local requirements.** Every county, and most cities, require a **local business tax receipt** under ch. 205 before you operate at a location. Many also require a **certificate of use** or occupancy showing the premises are zoned and built for the activity. A use not permitted in the zone cannot be cured by a license.

**Tax registrations.** A business selling taxable goods or services must register with the Department of Revenue before making taxable sales (§ 212.18). An employer must register for reemployment tax once it crosses the ch. 443 threshold, which is generally $1,500 of wages in a calendar quarter or one employee in 20 different weeks (verify) (§ 443.1215).

**Names.** An entity doing business under any name other than its exact registered name must file a fictitious name under § 865.09. The filing costs $50 (verify), lasts five years (verify), and requires one newspaper notice. Failure bars the business from maintaining a lawsuit until it registers, and is a second-degree misdemeanor (§ 865.09(9), (10)).

**Burden.** The agency bears the burden in a disciplinary case. In a civil case, the party asserting that a contract is unenforceable for lack of a license pleads and proves it as an affirmative defense.

## How courts reason about it

Courts sort licensing statutes into two kinds, and the sort drives the outcome.

**Regulatory versus revenue.** The old common-law line asked whether a license requirement protects the public (regulatory) or merely raises money (revenue). Contracts made in violation of a regulatory license are generally unenforceable by the unlicensed party. Contracts made without a revenue license are enforceable, and the penalty is only the fine. A local business tax receipt is a revenue measure. A contractor's license is a regulatory one. Florida follows this majority approach, and for contractors the Legislature codified it.

**Section 489.128.** The statute says a contract entered into by an unlicensed contractor "shall be unenforceable in law or in equity by the unlicensed contractor." Courts read that literally. The unlicensed contractor cannot sue on the contract, cannot recover in quantum meruit, and cannot foreclose a construction lien. The other party, by contrast, can sue the unlicensed contractor on the same contract. The Florida Supreme Court in *Earth Trades, Inc. v. T&G Corp.*, 108 So. 3d 580 (Fla. 2013) (verify citation), held that the owner's knowledge that the contractor was unlicensed is no defense. The policy is deterrence. Courts accept that some owners get free work and say the Legislature made that trade.

**What counts as "contracting."** Section 489.105(3) defines a contractor by the kind of work. A handyman who paints is not contracting. A handyman who moves a wall is. Courts look at the work actually performed, not the invoice label. A licensed contractor whose qualifying agent does not actually supervise can be treated as unlicensed for that job.

**Who the shield protects.** Courts ask whose interest the statute serves. Licensing acts protect consumers. So courts will not let an unlicensed provider use the statute as a sword, and will not let a client avoid paying a licensed provider over a paperwork lapse the statute does not make fatal.

**Civil consequences beyond the contract.** Section 768.0425 allows a consumer who suffers damages from unlicensed contracting to recover treble damages (verify). Section 489.127 makes unlicensed contracting a misdemeanor, a felony on a second offense or during a declared emergency (verify). Section 501.204 lets a court treat the unlicensed activity as an unfair or deceptive act under FDUTPA when the conduct misled the customer, which adds actual damages and fees.

## Florida specifics

- Florida follows the majority rule on regulatory versus revenue licenses, and codified the contractor result in § 489.128.
- A member or manager of an LLC who personally performs unlicensed contracting is personally criminally liable. The entity does not shield conduct that is itself a crime.
- Local business tax receipts are required by county and city under ch. 205; operating without one is a fine plus back tax, not contract unenforceability.
- An entity that is administratively dissolved for failing to file its annual report cannot maintain an action in Florida courts until reinstated (§ 605.0714 for LLCs) (verify section), though it can defend one. Reinstatement relates back.
- Unregistered foreign LLCs transacting business in Florida cannot maintain an action until they register (§ 605.0904). Contracts remain valid.
- A business operating under an unregistered fictitious name cannot maintain an action until it files (§ 865.09(9)). The cure is filing, and the case is abated, not dismissed.

## Case anchors

- [`cases/pnr-v-beacon-property-management.md`](../cases/pnr-v-beacon-property-management.md): FDUTPA reaches a single unfair act against a business, so an unlicensed provider's misstatements about its status can be a FDUTPA claim, not only a licensing violation.
- [`cases/sec-v-howey.md`](../cases/sec-v-howey.md): a regulatory regime applies based on what you actually did, not on what you called it. The citrus grove sellers did not think they were selling securities.
- [`cases/cantor-v-cochran.md`](../cases/cantor-v-cochran.md): whether a worker is an employee decides whether reemployment tax registration and workers' compensation are triggered.

## Angles checklist

- What does the business actually do, in the words a licensing statute would use? Check DBPR, FDACS, DOH, and OFR lists against each activity.
- Is the license personal to an individual, or does the entity hold it through a qualifying agent? Who is the qualifier, and are they actually supervising?
- Local: business tax receipt, certificate of use, zoning for this use at this address, signage, home occupation rules.
- Tax: sales tax registration before the first taxable sale; reemployment registration at the first employee.
- Name: fictitious name filed for every trade name used.
- Entity in good standing on Sunbiz; foreign registration if formed elsewhere.
- Flip it: in a contract dispute, is the other side licensed? If not, § 489.128 or the regulatory-license rule may make their claim disappear.
- Remedies: fine, discipline, criminal referral, unenforceability, treble damages, FDUTPA.

## Drill

Lena forms Coastal Reno LLC and starts doing kitchen remodels in Pinellas County. Her brother Marco has a certified general contractor license, and he agrees to "put the jobs under his license" for 10% of each job, though he never visits the sites. Coastal Reno signs a $48,000 remodel contract with a homeowner. Halfway through, the homeowner stops paying and says the tile work is defective. Coastal Reno wants to sue for the $24,000 balance and record a lien. Spot the issues.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- **Is Coastal Reno a licensed contractor?** No. The license belongs to Marco. An LLC can contract only through a qualifying agent who is actually responsible for the work (§ 489.119). Marco is not supervising and is not the LLC's qualifier. Renting a license does not make Coastal Reno licensed. A kitchen remodel that moves plumbing or electrical is contracting under § 489.105.
- **Section 489.128.** Coastal Reno cannot enforce the contract in law or equity. No contract claim, no quantum meruit, no lien foreclosure. The homeowner's knowledge, if any, is no defense under *Earth Trades*.
- **Lien.** Recording a lien an unlicensed contractor cannot enforce invites a fraudulent lien claim under § 713.31, with fees and punitive exposure.
- **Flip it.** The homeowner can sue Coastal Reno for the defective tile on the same contract, and may claim treble damages under § 768.0425 (verify) and a FDUTPA claim if Coastal Reno represented it was licensed. Lena is personally exposed under § 489.127.
- **Marco.** Disciplinary exposure for lending his license.
- **Facts that would change it.** If Marco were the properly designated qualifying agent for Coastal Reno and actually supervised, the LLC would be licensed and the case becomes an ordinary construction dispute over defective work, payment, and the lien.

</details>

## Check yourself

1. A bakery operates for two years without a local business tax receipt. A wholesale customer refuses to pay a $9,000 invoice and argues the contract is unenforceable. Who wins on that argument?
2. An LLC formed in Georgia sells into Florida, hires a Florida employee, and leases a Tampa office. It never registers as a foreign LLC. Can it sue a Florida customer in Florida?
3. Why does § 489.128 let the homeowner enforce the contract but not the contractor?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The bakery. A business tax receipt is a revenue measure under ch. 205. The penalty is the tax and a fine. It does not make contracts unenforceable.
2. Not until it registers under § 605.0904. The contract is still valid. The court will abate the case to allow registration. Back fees and penalties apply.
3. Because the statute protects the public, not the contractor. Making the contract one-sided deters unlicensed work while leaving the consumer every remedy. The Legislature accepted that some owners will get work without paying.

</details>
