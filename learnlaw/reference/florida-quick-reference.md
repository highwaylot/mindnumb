## Court system

| Court | What it hears | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| County court | Civil claims up to $50,000; small claims up to $8,000 (exclusive of interest, costs, fees) | 67 counties. Small claims uses the Florida Small Claims Rules, which are simpler and let a business appear through an officer or employee. |
| Circuit court | Civil claims over $50,000; equity; probate; family; felonies | 20 judicial circuits. A corporation or LLC must appear through a licensed attorney in circuit and county civil cases outside small claims. |
| District Courts of Appeal | Appeals from circuit and county courts | Six districts since January 1, 2023. Their decisions bind all Florida trial courts unless another DCA has ruled differently and the trial court is in that district. |
| Florida Supreme Court | Discretionary review, conflicts between DCAs, death penalty, bond validation, certified questions | Seven justices. |
| U.S. District Courts in Florida | Federal questions; diversity cases over $75,000 between citizens of different states | Northern, Middle, and Southern Districts. |
| U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit | Appeals from the three Florida districts, plus Georgia and Alabama | Sits in Atlanta. Its published opinions bind federal courts in Florida. |

## Tax facts (verify)

- Florida has no personal income tax. LLCs taxed as partnerships or disregarded entities, and S corporations, generally owe no Florida income tax.
- Florida corporate income tax: 5.5% on C corporations, with the first $50,000 exempt. An LLC that elects C corporation treatment pays it.
- Sales tax: 6% state rate plus a county discretionary surtax (0 to 2%, most counties 0.5 to 1.5%). Register with the Department of Revenue before making taxable sales.
- The sales tax on commercial rent was repealed effective October 1, 2025.
- Reemployment tax: paid by the employer on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages; new employers start at 2.7%.
- Workers' compensation is required for non-construction businesses with four or more employees, including corporate officers and LLC members who have not exempted out, and for construction businesses with one or more employees.

## Litigation numbers (verify)

- Diversity jurisdiction in federal court: amount in controversy over $75,000.
- Proposal for settlement (§ 768.79): a plaintiff who rejects a defendant's offer and recovers at least 25% less than it owes the defendant's fees from the offer date; a defendant who rejects a plaintiff's offer and is hit with a judgment at least 25% higher owes the plaintiff's fees.
- § 57.105 sanctions: 21-day safe harbor after service of the motion before it can be filed.
- Comparative fault: a plaintiff more than 50% at fault recovers nothing in a negligence case (§ 768.81(6), HB 837, 2023). Medical negligence is excepted.
- Usury: over 18% simple interest per year is usurious on loans of $500,000 or less; over 25% is criminal.
- Head of family wage garnishment: disposable earnings of $750 per week or less are fully exempt; above that, no garnishment without written consent.

## Non-compete numbers (§ 542.335 and the CHOICE Act, verify)

- Standard restrictive covenant against a former employee: 6 months or less presumed reasonable; more than 2 years presumed unreasonable.
- Against the seller of a business: 3 years or less presumed reasonable; more than 7 years presumed unreasonable.
- CHOICE Act (effective July 1, 2025): a "covered employee" earns more than twice the annual mean wage of the Florida county where the employer's principal place of business is. Covered non-competes and garden leave agreements can run up to 4 years, require 7 days to review and written notice of the right to counsel, and a court must preliminarily enjoin a breach.
