# Drill: Capstone, Harborline Supply

**Modules exercised:** all of them. **Time:** ~120 min

## Scenario

Harborline Supply LLC, formed in Sarasota in 2021, sells marine hardware online to customers in every state and installs docks, ladders, and lifts along the Gulf coast. Three members: Ana (50%, the sole manager), Ben (30%), and Cole (20%). The operating agreement is a template they downloaded. It makes the LLC manager-managed, says "no member shall engage in a competing business," and says nothing about buyouts, valuation, or what happens when a member leaves. Harborline has 14 W-2 employees and 6 installers paid on 1099s. The installers work full-time, on Harborline's schedule, in Harborline shirts, using Harborline trucks. Harborline rents a warehouse for $7,500 a month.

Since 2023 Harborline has bought stainless fittings under a two-year exclusive supply agreement with Tideline Hardware, a Georgia manufacturer. The contract fixes prices, requires Harborline to buy at least $200,000 a year, and renews automatically unless either side gives 90 days' notice. On October 1, 2026, Tideline emailed: "Effective immediately prices increase 30%. Confirm by Friday or we ship nothing."

Harborline has used the Harborline name online since 2021 and never registered it anywhere. A Georgia company, Harbor Line Marine, began selling similar products online in 2024, filed a federal trademark application in March 2026, and in September sent Harborline a cease-and-desist letter.

Sofia, the warehouse lead, paid a salary with no overtime for 50-hour weeks, emailed Ana on September 8, 2026 complaining about unpaid overtime. On September 15 she told Ana she was pregnant. On September 22 Ana fired her, saying "we need someone who can lift." Sofia's lawyer has written.

On September 10 an employee clicked a phishing link. The attacker was inside the order database for nine days. Exposed: names, emails, addresses, and last four card digits for 2,300 customers in 31 states, and full card numbers for 300 customers that an old checkout plugin had stored. Nobody has been notified.

Cole wants out. He demanded $400,000 for his 20% on October 5 and, Ben has learned, has been consulting for Harbor Line Marine since the summer.

In August a dock ladder installed by one of the 1099 installers at a customer's home pulled out of the piling. The customer broke an ankle. Her lawyer has sent a preservation letter.

Longboat Marinas, Inc. owes Harborline $85,000 for dock work finished in June under a written master services agreement that requires arbitration in Atlanta under Georgia law and awards fees to the prevailing party. Longboat says the work was late and has not paid.

Today is October 29, 2026.

## Your task

List every legal issue you can see. For each: who could claim what against whom, what rule governs, what the likely outcome is, and what facts you would want to know that the scenario does not give you.

Write your answer before opening the model answer.

**Steps 3 and 4, the doctrines and the flip, ranked.**

### Issue 1: The data breach and the notice clocks
- **Parties and claim:** The Florida Attorney General, 30 other states, affected customers, and the processor against Harborline.
- **Rule:** The Florida Information Protection Act requires notice to affected individuals within 30 days of determining a breach, notice to the Attorney General within 30 days when 500 or more Floridians are affected, and notice to credit agencies above 1,000; a 15-day extension is available for good cause (§ 501.171, verify). No private right of action, but violations are enforced as unfair practices with civil penalties. Every other affected state has its own statute and clock. The merchant agreement requires PCI compliance and prompt reporting of card compromise.
- **Application:** Nine days of access, full card numbers for 300, and no notice six weeks later. If "determination" was September 19, the Florida deadline has passed; if the extent was learned later, there may be days left.
- **Likely outcome:** The most urgent item. Forensic report, notices in 31 states, processor notification, credit monitoring for the 300. Penalties scale with delay.
- **Facts that would change it:** The date the breach was determined. Whether card numbers were encrypted. Whether a cyber policy exists.

### Issue 2: Sofia, retaliation and pregnancy
- **Parties and claim:** Sofia against Harborline and against Ana personally under the FLSA.
- **Rule:** The FLSA forbids discharging an employee for complaining about unpaid overtime, an informal written complaint counts, and remedies include back pay, liquidated damages, reinstatement or front pay, and fees (29 U.S.C. §§ 215(a)(3), 216(b)); individuals with operational control can be "employers." Title VII as amended by the Pregnancy Discrimination Act forbids firing because of pregnancy, and the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act requires reasonable accommodation of known limitations (42 U.S.C. §§ 2000e(k), 2000gg); both apply to employers with 15 or more employees. The Florida Civil Rights Act covers pregnancy at the same threshold (ch. 760). Florida's Private Whistleblower Act protects written objections to violations of law at employers with 10 or more employees (§§ 448.101–448.105). Florida has no common-law wrongful discharge claim, which is why the statutes matter (see [`cases/demarco-v-publix.md`](../cases/demarco-v-publix.md)).
- **Application:** Complaint, disclosure, termination, in fourteen days, with "someone who can lift" said out loud. Timing plus a statement about pregnancy is close to direct evidence. The 15-employee threshold is met if the installers are employees (Issue 3); 14 plus 6 is 20.
- **Likely outcome:** Strong claims. Settlement value is back pay, emotional distress, fees, and the risk of liquidated and punitive damages.
- **Facts that would change it:** Documented performance problems before September 8. Who decided, and when. Headcount by week.

### Issue 3: The six installers
- **Parties and claim:** The installers, the IRS, the Department of Revenue, and the Division of Workers' Compensation against Harborline; this issue also decides Issues 2, 4, and 11.
- **Rule:** Florida uses the right-to-control test (*Cantor v. Cochran*, 184 So. 2d 173 (Fla. 1966)); the FLSA uses economic reality. Full-time work on the hirer's schedule, in its uniform, with its equipment, doing its core business, is employment under both. Consequences: overtime and minimum wage; reemployment tax (ch. 443); workers' compensation, required for construction employers with one or more employees and for others with four or more (ch. 440, verify); payroll tax and penalties. See [`cases/cantor-v-cochran.md`](../cases/cantor-v-cochran.md).
- **Application:** These are employees in everything but the tax form.
- **Likely outcome:** Reclassification, back taxes, overtime exposure, and possibly uninsured-employer penalties (§ 440.107).
- **Facts that would change it:** Whether the installers have their own licenses, insurance, other clients, and the right to refuse work.

### Issue 4: The ladder injury
- **Parties and claim:** The customer against Harborline, the installer, and the ladder's manufacturer.
- **Rule:** Negligent installation is the installer's tort; Harborline answers for it under respondeat superior if he is an employee, and may answer anyway on an apparent-agency theory given the shirts and trucks, or because the duty it undertook by contract was its own. A seller in the distribution chain is strictly liable for a defective product (*West v. Caterpillar Tractor Co.*, 336 So. 2d 80 (Fla. 1976)), with indemnity against the manufacturer. Comparative fault applies, with the more-than-50% bar (§ 768.81(6)). Limitation 2 years (verify). Medical damages evidence is limited to amounts paid (§ 768.0427, verify).
- **Application:** Whether the piling, the hardware, or the installer failed decides who pays. Harborline is in the case either way.
- **Likely outcome:** Covered by general liability insurance if the policy is in force and installers are not excluded. Tender now. Preserve the ladder and the installer's records; the preservation letter makes spoliation a live risk.
- **Facts that would change it:** The failure mode. The customer's own conduct.

### Issue 5: Cole's competition and the fiduciary question
- **Parties and claim:** Harborline against Cole for breach of the operating agreement, breach of fiduciary duty, and trade secret misappropriation; expulsion.
- **Rule:** In a manager-managed LLC, a member who is not a manager owes no fiduciary duties solely by reason of being a member (§ 605.04091, verify subsection); the manager does. So the lever against Cole is the operating agreement's non-compete clause, which is a contract, and chapter 688 if he gave Harbor Line Marine customer data. A court may expel a member who willfully and persistently breaches the operating agreement or engages in wrongful conduct that materially harms the company (§ 605.0602(6), verify). See [`cases/meinhard-v-salmon.md`](../cases/meinhard-v-salmon.md) for the fiduciary baseline the Act narrows.
- **Application:** Consulting for a direct competitor that is also attacking the company's name is a plain breach of the clause. Whether it is also a trade secret case depends on what he took.
- **Likely outcome:** Leverage. Expulsion, damages, and injunction are available; practically, Cole's breach reshapes the buyout.
- **Facts that would change it:** What Cole shared. Whether the clause is reasonable under § 542.335, which applies to covenants among owners too.

### Issue 6: Cole's exit
- **Parties and claim:** Cole against Harborline for a buyout; Harborline's alternatives.
- **Rule:** A member may dissociate at will (§ 605.0601) but then holds only a transferable interest in distributions (§§ 605.0603, 605.0502). There is no statutory buyout. Cole's path to cash is a petition for judicial dissolution (§ 605.0702), which the company or other members can answer by electing to buy his interest at fair value set by the court (§ 605.0706). The operating agreement's silence means the default rules govern.
- **Application:** Cole has no right to $400,000 or to any number. Harborline cannot force him out at a discount either, absent expulsion. Both sides end up at a valuation.
- **Likely outcome:** A negotiated purchase, discounted for Cole's breach and lack of marketability, with a release and a reaffirmed non-compete.
- **Facts that would change it:** The company's value. Whether Cole has creditors, which would make a charging order (§ 605.0503) the only remedy against his interest.

### Issue 7: Tideline's price ultimatum
- **Parties and claim:** Harborline against Tideline for breach and anticipatory repudiation; Tideline against Harborline on the minimum purchase and exclusivity.
- **Rule:** A sale of goods is governed by UCC Article 2 (ch. 672). A fixed-price contract cannot be changed unilaterally; a clear refusal to perform except on new terms is a repudiation (§ 672.610). The buyer may demand adequate assurance in writing and suspend performance (§ 672.609), then cover by buying substitute goods and recover the difference plus incidental and consequential damages (§§ 672.711, 672.712, 672.715). Increased cost alone is not commercial impracticability (§ 672.615). Consequential damages must have been foreseeable at contracting ([`cases/hadley-v-baxendale.md`](../cases/hadley-v-baxendale.md)).
- **Application:** "Confirm or we ship nothing" is a repudiation. Harborline may cover despite the exclusivity clause, because Tideline's breach excuses it, and should give the 90-day non-renewal notice on time if it wants out at the end of the term.
- **Likely outcome:** Good position on the merits; the practical problem is supply continuity.
- **Facts that would change it:** Any price-adjustment, force majeure, limitation-of-remedies, or forum clause. Whether a tariff drove the increase and who bears it under the contract.

### Issue 8: The trademark fight
- **Parties and claim:** Harbor Line Marine against Harborline, and the reverse, for infringement and unfair competition.
- **Rule:** Trademark rights come from use. The senior user in a territory keeps its rights against a later federal registrant in that territory (15 U.S.C. § 1115(b)(5)), and an application's constructive-use priority runs only from the filing date (§ 1057(c)). Likelihood of confusion is the test for infringement (§ 1125(a)). Anyone who believes it would be damaged can oppose an application within 30 days of publication (§ 1063). Florida registration under ch. 495 adds state remedies but not priority.
- **Application:** Harborline has used the name nationwide online since 2021, three years before the competitor existed. It is the senior user. The cease-and-desist has it backwards.
- **Likely outcome:** Oppose the application, file its own, and answer the letter with priority evidence. The risk is proof: dated screenshots, invoices, and ads from 2021 onward.
- **Facts that would change it:** The competitor's actual first-use date. Whether Harborline's early sales were interstate.

### Issue 9: Sofia's overtime
- **Parties and claim:** Sofia against Harborline and Ana for unpaid overtime.
- **Rule:** Salary alone does not make an employee exempt; the duties must fit an exemption. A warehouse lead who mostly does manual work is non-exempt. Overtime is owed at time and a half over 40 hours, with liquidated damages and fees, for 2 years or 3 if willful (29 U.S.C. §§ 207, 216(b), 255).
- **Application:** Ten hours a week for her tenure, doubled.
- **Likely outcome:** Owed. It also proves the protected activity for Issue 2.
- **Facts that would change it:** Her actual duties and supervisory role.

### Issue 10: The $85,000 Longboat receivable
- **Parties and claim:** Harborline against Longboat for breach of the MSA; Longboat's delay defense and set-off.
- **Rule:** A written arbitration clause is enforced under the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. § 2) and the Revised Florida Arbitration Code (ch. 682). Forum and choice-of-law clauses in a commercial contract are generally honored. The prevailing-party fee clause applies in arbitration. Written contract claims carry a 5-year limitation period (verify).
- **Application:** The claim must be arbitrated in Atlanta under Georgia law. Delay damages are Longboat's set-off only if the MSA made time of the essence or fixed liquidated damages.
- **Likely outcome:** An award for most of the $85,000, less proven delay damages, plus fees; then confirmation where Longboat has assets.
- **Facts that would change it:** The MSA's schedule and notice provisions. Whether the delay complaint predates the invoice.

### Issue 11: Workers' compensation gap
- **Parties and claim:** The Division of Workers' Compensation against Harborline; any injured installer against Harborline in tort.
- **Rule:** Dock installation is construction work, for which coverage is required with one or more employees (ch. 440, verify classification). An employer that fails to secure coverage faces stop-work orders and penalties (§ 440.107) and loses the tort immunity that compensation coverage provides (§ 440.11).
- **Application:** If the installers are employees and uncovered, every day of work is exposure.
- **Likely outcome:** Secure coverage now; the cost is small next to an uninsured injury.
- **Facts that would change it:** The current policy and its classifications.

### Issue 12: Stored card data and PCI
- **Parties and claim:** The processor and card networks against Harborline under the merchant agreement.
- **Rule:** Storing full card numbers through an unmaintained plugin violates the PCI standard incorporated in the merchant agreement; the remedies are fines, forensic audits, and termination.
- **Application:** The 300 full numbers turn an ordinary breach into a card-compromise event.
- **Likely outcome:** Fines are likely; termination is possible. Remove the plugin and tokenize.
- **Facts that would change it:** Whether the processor was notified and what the agreement requires.

### Issue 13: FDUTPA and the privacy policy
- **Parties and claim:** Customers and the Attorney General against Harborline.
- **Rule:** Promising security practices you do not follow is a deceptive act (§ 501.204). FDUTPA allows actual damages and fees for a prevailing party (§ 501.2105); the Attorney General can seek penalties.
- **Application:** Most privacy policies promise "industry-standard security." Harborline's did not deliver it.
- **Likely outcome:** Class-action risk layered on the breach, limited by the difficulty of proving actual damages.
- **Facts that would change it:** The privacy policy text.

### Issue 14: Ana's personal exposure
- **Parties and claim:** Sofia and the installers against Ana personally.
- **Rule:** Members are not liable for LLC obligations (§ 605.0304), and piercing requires improper conduct (*Dania Jai-Alai Palace, Inc. v. Sykes*, 450 So. 2d 1114 (Fla. 1984)). But the FLSA makes an individual with operational control an "employer," and anyone who personally commits a tort is liable for it. See [`cases/dania-jai-alai-v-sykes.md`](../cases/dania-jai-alai-v-sykes.md).
- **Application:** Ana set pay and fired Sofia. The FLSA claims reach her. The discrimination statutes and the general liability claims do not.
- **Likely outcome:** Real but bounded personal exposure on wage claims.
- **Facts that would change it:** Who controlled payroll decisions.

### Issue 15: Ana's duties to Ben and Cole
- **Parties and claim:** Ben or Cole against Ana, directly or derivatively.
- **Rule:** As the sole manager Ana owes the company and members the duties of loyalty and care (§ 605.04091); care is breached only by gross negligence, recklessness, willful misconduct, or knowing violation of law. Derivative claims require a demand or an excuse (§ 605.0802). The business judgment rule protects informed, good-faith decisions.
- **Application:** Misclassification, no workers' compensation, no breach response, and a retaliatory firing are knowing violations outside the protected zone.
- **Likely outcome:** Weak as a standalone suit, strong as Cole's counter-leverage in the buyout.
- **Facts that would change it:** Whether Ana was advised and ignored it.

### Issue 16: Licensing and permits for dock work
- **Parties and claim:** DBPR, the county, and environmental regulators against Harborline.
- **Rule:** Dock and seawall construction is licensed at the state or local level in much of Florida (verify the scope for Sarasota and Manatee Counties), and work over water usually needs Department of Environmental Protection or Army Corps authorization. An unlicensed contractor's contracts are unenforceable by it (§ 489.128).
- **Application:** If Harborline needed a license it did not hold, Longboat has a defense to the $85,000 claim and the injured customer has another theory.
- **Likely outcome:** A fact to confirm before arbitration is filed.
- **Facts that would change it:** Harborline's licenses and the permit record for the Longboat job.

### Red herrings
- Corporate Transparency Act reporting. FinCEN exempted domestic companies in March 2025 (verify).
- E-Verify. Required for private employers with 25 or more employees (§ 448.095); even at 20, Harborline is under it.
- Sales tax on the warehouse rent. Repealed effective October 1, 2025.
- "Sofia was at-will." At-will employment is no defense to statutory retaliation or discrimination.
- "They signed 1099s, so they are contractors." The form does not decide status.
- Suing Tideline in tort for the price increase. It is a contract case; the independent tort requirement bars a negligence theory between contracting parties.
- Piercing the veil to reach Ben. He did nothing and manages nothing.
- "Cole has to sell at book value." The agreement says nothing, so no formula applies.
- Suing the phisher. Uncollectible and beside the point; the duties run to customers and regulators.

### Procedural and remedial angles
- Breach: regulatory, not a lawsuit yet; notices to individuals, the Attorney General, other states, the processor, and the cyber carrier.
- Sofia: EEOC charge within 300 days and FCHR within 365 (verify) before any Title VII or FCRA suit; FLSA and whistleblower claims can be filed directly in the Middle District of Florida or Sarasota County circuit court (Twelfth Judicial Circuit). Fees shift to a prevailing employee. Early mediation is standard.
- Installers: a payroll audit, voluntary reclassification, and the IRS settlement program (verify availability); workers' compensation coverage immediately.
- Injury: circuit court, over $50,000 (verify); tender to the general liability carrier; proposal for settlement under § 768.79 once the medical picture is clear; 2-year limitation (verify).
- Cole: demand letter citing the non-compete, a records demand under § 605.0410 from his side, mediation, then a dissolution petition and § 605.0706 election if talks fail. Circuit court, equitable.
- Tideline: written demand for adequate assurance under § 672.609, then cover; check the contract for forum and arbitration clauses; 4-year limitation (§ 672.725).
- Trademark: notice of opposition at the Trademark Trial and Appeal Board within 30 days of publication, extensions available; federal application for Harborline; a Lanham Act claim in federal court if confusion is occurring.
- Longboat: demand for arbitration in Atlanta under the MSA; fees to the prevailing party; confirm and domesticate the award in Florida under ch. 55 if Longboat's assets are here; 5-year limitation (verify).
- Overall: the ranked list is the agenda for the first meeting with counsel.
