# Forming a Florida LLC

**Module:** 02 · **Lesson:** 2 · **Time:** ~45 min
**Tags:** `[FL]`

## Why this matters to a business owner

Forming an LLC is cheap and takes an afternoon. Forming it so that it actually protects you takes a few more steps that most online services skip: a real operating agreement, separate finances, a registered agent who will be there when the process server arrives, and an annual report filed on time. The formation document is public and permanent. What you put in it, and what you leave out, is the first evidence a court sees about what kind of company this is.

## The rule

A Florida LLC exists when the Department of State files its Articles of Organization, § 605.0201, Fla. Stat. Everything else is either required to keep it alive or recommended to make it work. The sequence:

1. **Pick a name.** It must contain "limited liability company," "LLC," or "L.L.C.," and must be distinguishable on the Department's records from every other registered name, § 605.0112. Search Sunbiz first. Registering an entity name does not give trademark rights; module 06 covers that.
2. **Choose a registered agent.** Every LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent with a Florida street address who is available during business hours to accept service of process, § 605.0113. The agent signs an acceptance in the articles. The agent can be a member, a Florida resident, or a company in the business of serving as agent. A P.O. box does not work.
3. **File Articles of Organization.** The articles must state the name, the principal office and mailing address, the registered agent's name, address, and acceptance, and the name and address of each person authorized to manage if manager-managed, § 605.0201. The fee is $125 (verify): $100 filing plus $25 registered agent designation. File online at Sunbiz. The articles may set a delayed effective date up to 90 days (verify) after filing, § 605.0207. Existence begins when the articles take effect.
4. **Get an EIN.** The Employer Identification Number from the IRS is free and immediate online using Form SS-4. Banks require it, and so does payroll. Even a single-member LLC with no employees should have one so the owner's Social Security number stays off business paperwork.
5. **Adopt an operating agreement.** Not filed and not required by statute, but the document that decides everything the articles do not: who owns what, who decides, how money moves, what happens on exit. Florida recognizes oral and implied operating agreements, § 605.0102(45), which is a reason to have a written one. Lesson 3.
6. **Open a business bank account** and run every dollar through it. Capital contributions go in; distributions come out; personal expenses never touch it.
7. **Register for taxes and licenses.** The Department of Revenue for sales tax if you sell taxable goods or services and for reemployment tax once you have employees; the county and city for a local business tax receipt under ch. 205; the Department of Business and Professional Regulation or another agency if the trade is licensed. Module 07.
8. **Register any fictitious name.** If the LLC does business under any name other than its exact legal name, register it under § 865.09 for $50 (verify), good for five years, after publishing one notice in a newspaper in the county of the principal place of business.
9. **File the annual report.** Due between January 1 and May 1 each year, $138.75 (verify). A $400 (verify) late fee applies after May 1, and the Department administratively dissolves the LLC if the report is not filed by the third Friday in September, § 605.0714. Reinstatement is possible under § 605.0715 on payment of all fees and penalties, and relates back as if the dissolution never happened.
10. **Qualify in other states if needed.** An LLC that does business in another state must register there as a foreign LLC. Out-of-state LLCs doing business in Florida register under § 605.0902; the penalty for not doing so includes inability to sue in Florida courts until cured, § 605.0904.

Management form is chosen in the articles or operating agreement. The default is **member-managed**: every member has authority in the ordinary course. **Manager-managed** vests authority in named managers, § 605.0407. An LLC may file a **statement of authority** under § 605.0302 to tell the world who can sign for it.

## How courts reason about it

Formation questions reach courts in three ways.

**Was the entity formed when the deal was made?** A person who signs a contract for an LLC that does not yet exist is personally liable on it under general agency and promoter principles; the later-formed LLC is bound only if it adopts the contract. Courts look at the filing date on Sunbiz and the signature block. "Pawsitively Clean LLC" with no filing is a trade name for whoever signed. The reasoning is that the other party had no entity to look to, so it must have been relying on the person.

**Who had authority?** In a member-managed LLC, each member is an agent for ordinary-course acts, and third parties may rely on that unless they know otherwise. In a manager-managed LLC, a non-manager member has no authority to bind. Courts enforce the structure the public record shows, which is why the articles should match how the business actually runs. The statement of authority exists so an LLC can expand or limit that reliance on the record.

**Does the lapse matter?** Administrative dissolution for a missed annual report is common. Courts treat a dissolved LLC as continuing to exist for winding up, and reinstatement is retroactive, § 605.0715(3) (verify). A contract signed during the lapse is generally enforceable once the LLC is reinstated. But a member who carries on new business during dissolution, knowing of it, risks personal liability, and the name becomes available to others after dissolution. The reasoning is to encourage cure without letting lapses become a defense to the company's own obligations.

On the shield itself, Florida's statute says that failing to observe formalities is not a ground for personal liability, § 605.0304(2). Courts therefore do not ask whether the LLC held meetings. They ask whether the owners used it as a separate thing: separate accounts, contracts in the company name, capital adequate for its ordinary risks. Those facts come from the formation habits above. Lesson 5.

## Florida specifics

- Florida's filing facts are in the fact sheet. Verify every fee and date on Sunbiz before relying on them.
- Florida does not require a published notice of LLC formation, unlike some states, and does not require the operating agreement or the names of members to be filed. Members' names appear on Sunbiz only if listed in the articles or annual report as managers or authorized persons.
- Single-member LLCs are fully recognized and get the same liability shield, § 605.0304, but weaker protection against the member's own creditors under § 605.0503(4). Lesson 6.
- Florida enacted protected series LLC provisions in 2025 with an effective date of July 1, 2026 (verify current status). Before that, Florida LLCs could not form series.
- An existing Florida corporation or partnership can **convert** into an LLC under §§ 605.1041 and following, and an out-of-state LLC can **domesticate** to Florida under §§ 605.1051 and following (verify sections), without dissolving and reforming.
- Members' names and addresses are public once listed, and the annual report requires at least one manager or authorized person. Many owners use a registered agent service address as the principal office to keep a home address off the record.
- The beneficial ownership report under the federal Corporate Transparency Act is no longer required for U.S.-formed companies after FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule (verify).

## Angles checklist

- Is the LLC on Sunbiz as active? When was it formed relative to the contract or event in question?
- Who is the registered agent, and is the address current and staffed?
- Is it member-managed or manager-managed, and did the signer have authority?
- Is there a written operating agreement, and does it match the articles?
- Is there a separate bank account, and have personal and business funds stayed apart?
- Has every year's annual report been filed? Any lapse and reinstatement?
- Does it trade under any unregistered name?
- Does it do business in other states without registering there?
- Has it registered with the Department of Revenue and obtained local and professional licenses?

## Drill

Priya files Articles of Organization for "Coastal Tile LLC" on March 3, listing herself as registered agent at her home. She signs a $40,000 tile supply contract on February 20 as "Coastal Tile LLC, by Priya, Owner." She never writes an operating agreement, pays the supplier from her personal account, and misses the next year's annual report. In October of that year a process server cannot find anyone at her home during the day. The supplier sues for the unpaid balance and names Priya personally. List the formation issues.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- **Pre-formation contract.** The LLC did not exist on February 20. Priya is personally liable on the contract unless the supplier agreed to look only to the future entity, which the signature block does not show. The LLC may have adopted the contract by performing it, making both liable.
- **Registered agent.** Priya's home with no one present during business hours fails § 605.0113. The supplier can use substituted service under § 48.062, and a default may follow that she never sees.
- **Administrative dissolution.** Missing the annual report brings a $400 late fee after May 1 and dissolution after the third Friday in September. Contracts during the lapse remain enforceable against the LLC, and reinstatement is retroactive, but the lapse is a bad fact.
- **Commingling.** Paying business debts from a personal account is not a formality failure, which § 605.0304(2) excuses. It is evidence the LLC was not operated as a separate entity, which goes to the improper-use element under *Dania Jai-Alai*. By itself it is probably not enough to pierce; combined with other misuse, it might be.
- **No operating agreement.** Irrelevant to the supplier, but it means ch. 605 defaults govern every internal question.
- **Open questions.** Did the supplier know the LLC was not yet formed? Did the LLC ratify the contract after March 3? Has Priya reinstated?

</details>

## Check yourself

1. Can a Florida LLC use its registered agent's address as its principal office?
2. An LLC is administratively dissolved in September for a missed report and signs a lease in November. Is the lease void?
3. Why get an EIN for a single-member LLC with no employees?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Yes, if the agent agrees. The articles require a principal office address and a registered agent address; they may be the same. The registered agent address must be a Florida street address.
2. No. A dissolved LLC continues for winding up, and reinstatement under § 605.0715 relates back. The lease is generally enforceable. The members risk personal exposure if they knowingly carried on new business during dissolution, and the LLC should reinstate promptly.
3. To open a business bank account, to keep the owner's Social Security number off vendor and customer paperwork, and to be ready for payroll. It also reinforces that the LLC is a separate taxpayer identity even when disregarded for income tax.

</details>
