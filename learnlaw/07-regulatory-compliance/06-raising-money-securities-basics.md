# Raising money: securities basics

**Module:** 07 · **Lesson:** 6 · **Time:** ~50 min
**Tags:** `[US]` `[FL]`

## Why this matters to a business owner

The moment you take money from someone who will not work in the business and expects a return, you have probably sold a security, whatever you call it. Securities law then requires registration, which no small business does, or an exemption, which has rules. Getting it wrong gives every investor the right to demand their money back with interest, no matter how well the business did. The anti-fraud rules apply regardless.

## The rule

**What is a security.** The federal and Florida statutes list stocks, bonds, notes, and "investment contracts." *SEC v. W.J. Howey Co.*, 328 U.S. 293 (1946), defines an investment contract by four elements: (1) an investment of money, (2) in a common enterprise, (3) with an expectation of profits, (4) derived from the entrepreneurial or managerial efforts of others. Substance controls over form. Florida's definition in § 517.021 tracks it and Florida courts apply *Howey*.

**LLC interests.** An interest in a manager-managed LLC sold to a passive member is a security. An interest in a member-managed LLC where each member actually participates may not be, under the *Williamson v. Tucker*, 645 F.2d 404 (5th Cir. 1981), analysis, which is binding in the Eleventh Circuit. The questions are whether the member has real power and the experience to use it.

**Notes.** A promissory note is presumed to be a security unless it resembles a list of commercial exceptions, under *Reves v. Ernst & Young*, 494 U.S. 56 (1990). Notes sold to raise capital from investors are in. SAFEs and convertible notes are securities.

**Registration or exemption.** Section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933 bars sale of an unregistered security absent an exemption. The exemptions small businesses use:

- **Rule 506(b) of Regulation D.** No general solicitation or advertising. Unlimited accredited investors and up to 35 (verify) non-accredited investors who are sophisticated and receive specified disclosure. File Form D with the SEC within 15 days (verify) of the first sale.
- **Rule 506(c).** General solicitation allowed, but every purchaser must be accredited and the issuer must take reasonable steps to verify it, not merely accept a checkbox.
- **Rule 504.** Up to $10 million (verify) in 12 months; state law governs solicitation and investor rules.
- **Regulation Crowdfunding.** Up to $5 million (verify) in 12 months through an SEC-registered funding portal, with Form C disclosure and per-investor limits for non-accredited investors.

**Accredited investor (Rule 501(a)).** An individual with income over $200,000 (verify) in each of the last two years, $300,000 (verify) with a spouse, with a reasonable expectation of the same; or net worth over $1 million (verify) excluding the primary residence; or holding certain securities licenses. Also entities with over $5 million (verify) in assets.

**Florida, ch. 517.** Section 517.07 requires registration unless exempt. Rule 506 offerings are "covered securities" under federal law, so Florida cannot require registration; Florida does not require a notice filing for them (verify). For other offerings, § 517.061 exemptions apply, including the limited offering exemption in § 517.061(11): no more than 35 (verify) purchasers in Florida in 12 months, no general solicitation, and a three-day (verify) rescission right disclosed to each purchaser.

**Anti-fraud.** Rule 10b-5 and § 517.301 prohibit any untrue statement of material fact, or omission of a fact needed to make what was said not misleading, in connection with the sale of a security. They apply to every sale, exempt or not, including to friends and family.

**Who sells.** Paying a finder a commission to bring investors requires the finder to be a registered broker-dealer under § 517.12 and federal law.

**Remedies.** Under § 12(a)(1) of the Securities Act, a purchaser of an unregistered, non-exempt security may rescind and recover the price plus interest, within one year (verify) of the sale, without proving fault. Florida's § 517.211 gives rescission for sales in violation of § 517.07 or § 517.301, with joint liability for directors, officers, managers, and agents who personally participated, and attorney fees.

**Burden.** The issuer bears the burden of proving the exemption. The investor bears the burden on anti-fraud elements.

## How courts reason about it

- **Efforts of others.** The fourth *Howey* element is where LLC cases are decided. Courts ask whether the investor's expected return depends on the promoter's work. A passive member who votes once a year is relying on others. A member who runs the kitchen is not. Labels matter less than what the member actually does.
- **General solicitation.** Any public announcement, website, social media post, or pitch to strangers is general solicitation and destroys 506(b). Courts look for a pre-existing substantive relationship between the issuer and each offeree.
- **Integration.** Multiple small rounds that are really one offering are integrated and tested together. The SEC's 2021 framework uses a 30-day safe harbor between offerings (verify).
- **Materiality and projections.** Anti-fraud liability turns on what a reasonable investor would consider important. Optimistic projections are protected if they have a reasonable basis and are accompanied by meaningful cautionary language.
- **Control person liability.** Courts hold managers, members, and officers who participated in the sale jointly liable with the entity under § 517.211 and § 15 of the Securities Act. The LLC shield does not apply to the person's own participation in an unlawful sale.
- **Rescission value.** Courts enforce rescission mechanically. A business that raised and spent $300,000 owes $300,000 plus interest if an investor elects rescission in time.

## Florida specifics

- Florida follows the majority of states and federal law in using *Howey* and in deferring to federal preemption for Rule 506 offerings.
- Florida's Office of Financial Regulation enforces ch. 517 and can bring administrative actions and refer criminal cases; willful violations are felonies under § 517.302.
- Florida's rescission remedy in § 517.211 is broader than the federal remedy because it adds attorney fees and extends to agents who participated.
- The limited offering exemption's three-day rescission right must actually be disclosed in writing; failing to disclose it defeats the exemption.

## Angles checklist

- Who is putting in money, and will they work in the business with real authority?
- What instrument: units, notes, SAFE, revenue share? Each is likely a security.
- How were investors found? Any public post, ad, or stranger pitch?
- Are all investors accredited? How was that verified and documented?
- Which exemption, and is every condition met: investor count, disclosure, Form D, Florida filing or exemption notice?
- What was said, written, or projected? Is there a disclosure document with risk factors?
- Who was paid to find investors, and are they registered?
- Flip it: can any investor rescind, and for how long? Who is personally liable?
- Fiduciary and operating agreement angles: what rights did the investor get, and what duties does the manager owe?

## Drill

Dev founds Keys Kombucha LLC, manager-managed, and needs $250,000. He posts on LinkedIn: "Raising a seed round, DM me." Twelve people invest $20,000 each in Class B non-voting units: his aunt, three college friends, and eight strangers from LinkedIn. He tells them "we'll be in Publix by spring" based on one meeting with a buyer. He pays a friend $5,000 for introducing four of them. Fourteen months later the Publix deal has not happened and two investors want out. Spot the issues.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- **Security.** Non-voting units in a manager-managed LLC sold to passive investors are investment contracts under *Howey*.
- **Exemption.** The LinkedIn post is general solicitation, so 506(b) is unavailable. 506(c) requires all accredited investors with verification; nothing suggests either. Florida's § 517.061(11) also bars general solicitation. There is likely no exemption, so the sales violated § 5 and § 517.07.
- **Rescission.** Under § 517.211, each investor may rescind and recover $20,000 plus interest and attorney fees. Dev is personally liable as the manager who made the sales. The federal one-year period may have run for the earliest sales; Florida's period is longer (verify).
- **Anti-fraud.** "We'll be in Publix by spring" stated as fact after one meeting is at least a reckless misstatement. Rescission under § 517.301 needs no exemption analysis.
- **Finder.** The $5,000 commission to an unregistered person violates § 517.12..
- **Aunt and friends.** No exception for family. They are purchasers with the same rights.
- **Fiduciary.** As manager, Dev owes the members duties under § 605.04091 and information rights under § 605.0410.
- **Facts that would change it.** If Dev had raised only from pre-existing relationships, with a private placement memorandum, Form D, and documented accreditation, the exemption would hold and only the Publix statement would remain as an anti-fraud issue.

</details>

## Check yourself

1. Three friends form a member-managed LLC, each investing $50,000 and each working full time in it. Securities?
2. Why does general solicitation matter so much under Rule 506(b)?
3. An investor rescinds under § 517.211 after the business failed. The money is gone. Who pays?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Probably not. Each member's return depends on their own efforts and they hold real power. If one later goes passive while the others run it, the analysis for that member can change.
2. Because 506(b)'s premise is a private offering to people with whom the issuer has a relationship. Public solicitation converts it into a public offering that needs either registration, 506(c) with accredited-only verified investors, or crowdfunding.
3. The LLC, and jointly the managers, officers, directors, and agents who personally participated in the sale. Personal liability under § 517.211(2) is why founders carry this risk individually.

</details>
