# Collecting a judgment

**Module:** 08 · **Lesson:** 6 · **Time:** ~50 min
**Tags:** `[FL]`

## Why this matters to a business owner

A judgment is a piece of paper. Nothing in it makes anyone pay. Collection is a second case with its own tools, costs, and exemptions, and many judgments are never collected. Ask the collectability question before suing. After winning, these tools turn paper into money. From the other side, they are what a creditor can do to you.

## The rule

**The judgment itself.** A final judgment must state the debtor's address (§ 55.01(2)) (verify). It lasts 20 years (verify) (§ 95.11(1)) and accrues interest at the rate set quarterly by the Chief Financial Officer under § 55.03, applied to the judgment from entry.

**Lien on real property.** A certified copy of the judgment recorded in the official records of a county becomes a lien on all real property the debtor owns or later acquires in that county, for 10 years (verify), renewable once by re-recording for a total of 20 (§ 55.10). Homestead property is exempt from the lien (lesson 7).

**Lien on personal property.** File a judgment lien certificate with the Florida Department of State (§§ 55.202 to 55.209). The lien attaches to the debtor's personal property in Florida, lasts five years (verify), and can be renewed once (verify). Priority runs by filing date. Without the certificate there is no lien.

**Writ of execution.** The clerk issues a writ (§ 56.021) directing the sheriff to levy on the debtor's non-exempt property and sell it. The creditor identifies the property and advances the sheriff's costs. Auctions yield little.

**Garnishment, ch. 77.** A writ served on a third party who holds the debtor's money, such as a bank or an employer. A continuing writ of garnishment against salary (§ 77.0305) takes a share of each paycheck until paid. The debtor receives notice and may claim exemptions within 20 days (verify) (§ 77.041). The **head of family exemption**, § 222.11, is the main one: a person who provides more than half the support of a child or other dependent has fully exempt disposable earnings up to $750 (verify) per week, and above that only if the person agreed to garnishment in writing. Federal law caps garnishment at 25% of disposable earnings for everyone else.

**Other exemptions.** Personal property to $1,000 (verify) (Fla. Const. art. X, § 4(a)(2)), a motor vehicle to $5,000 (verify) (§ 222.25(1)), an additional $4,000 (verify) if the debtor does not claim homestead (§ 222.25(4)), retirement accounts (§ 222.21), annuities and life insurance cash value (§ 222.14), and the homestead.

**Charging order, § 605.0503.** A judgment creditor of an LLC member may obtain a charging order, which is a lien on the member's transferable interest and gives the creditor the distributions the member would receive. For a multi-member LLC it is the exclusive remedy. For a single-member LLC, if distributions will not satisfy the judgment in a reasonable time, the court may order the interest foreclosed and sold, and the buyer becomes the member.

**Discovery in aid of execution.** Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.560 lets the creditor use every discovery tool after judgment. The judgment can require the debtor to complete a **Fact Information Sheet** (Form 1.977) within 45 days (verify), listing employers, accounts, property, and vehicles under oath.

**Proceedings supplementary, § 56.29.** On a motion showing an unsatisfied judgment and an unreturned or unsatisfied writ, the court can examine the debtor, implead third parties who hold the debtor's property or received transfers, and order property applied to the judgment.

**Fraudulent transfers, ch. 726.** The Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act. A transfer made with actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud a creditor (§ 726.105(1)(a), proved by "badges of fraud" in § 726.105(2) such as transfer to an insider, retention of control, and insolvency) or made for less than reasonably equivalent value while insolvent (§§ 726.105(1)(b), 726.106) is voidable. The action must be brought within four years (verify) of the transfer, or one year after discovery for actual fraud (§ 726.110).

**Out-of-state judgments.** Domesticated under §§ 55.501 to 55.509 by recording a certified copy with an affidavit. Federal judgments register under 28 U.S.C. § 1963.

**Burden.** The creditor proves the judgment and identifies the property. The debtor proves any exemption. In fraudulent transfer, the creditor proves the elements; the transferee proves good faith and value.

## How courts reason about it

- **Exemptions are liberally construed for the debtor.** Courts resolve doubts about head-of-family status and homestead in the debtor's favor.
- **But not for fraud.** Courts unwind transfers readily when the badges line up: a transfer to a spouse or a new LLC shortly after the debt arose, for no consideration, while the debtor kept using the property. Timing relative to the claim is the most weighted fact. Section 222.30 makes a conversion of non-exempt assets into exempt ones with intent to defraud voidable (verify).
- **Charging orders are read as written.** Courts apply the single-member foreclosure remedy as the statute provides and treat the multi-member charging order as exclusive even when the other members are family.
- **Garnishees are liable.** A bank or employer that ignores a served writ owes the creditor what it should have held.
- **Proceedings supplementary are flexible.** Courts use § 56.29 to reach property held by alter-ego entities and transferees without a separate lawsuit, with notice and a hearing for the impleaded party.

## Florida specifics

- Florida follows the majority of states in adopting the Uniform Fraudulent Transfer Act and a judgment lien registry. It departs in the breadth of its exemptions, especially the unlimited-value homestead and the head-of-family wage exemption.
- Judgment interest in Florida is simple, not compound, and is set quarterly (§ 55.03). Recent rates have been in the 8 to 10 percent range (verify).
- A judgment lien on real property requires the creditor's address on the recorded judgment or an affidavit; omitting it defeats the lien (§ 55.10(1)).

## Case anchors

- [`cases/olmstead-v-ftc.md`](../cases/olmstead-v-ftc.md): the Florida Supreme Court allowed a creditor to seize a single-member LLC interest, which led the Legislature to write § 605.0503's two-tier rule.
- [`cases/beal-bank-v-almand.md`](../cases/beal-bank-v-almand.md): property held by spouses as tenants by the entireties is beyond the reach of one spouse's creditor; the case explains the presumption.
- [`cases/dania-jai-alai-v-sykes.md`](../cases/dania-jai-alai-v-sykes.md): to collect an LLC's judgment from its owner, the creditor must prove improper conduct, not just undercapitalization.

## Angles checklist

- Before suing: what does the defendant own, where, and in whose name? Entity with assets or a shell?
- Judgment: debtor's address, creditor's address, correct legal name, certified copies ordered?
- Liens: judgment lien certificate filed, certified copy recorded where there is real property?
- Information: fact information sheet ordered, post-judgment discovery served, public records checked?
- Garnishment: which bank, which employer, head-of-family status likely?
- LLC interests: charging order, and foreclosure if single-member?
- Transfers: anything moved to insiders since the dispute began? Badges of fraud?
- Exemptions the debtor will claim: homestead, wages, vehicle, retirement, entireties.
- Flip it, as debtor: what is exempt, what is not, and is any planned transfer a fraudulent transfer?
- Cost: sheriff's fees and attorney time against the amount actually recoverable.

## Drill

Bay Electric LLC wins a $95,000 judgment against Rivera Builders LLC and its sole member, Luis, who personally guaranteed the contract. After the suit was filed, Luis transferred his truck to his brother for $1, moved his business accounts into a new LLC called Rivera Construction Group, and has his wife paid as the "manager" while he draws no salary. He and his wife own their home jointly. He has a 401(k). Spot the collection issues.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- **Liens first.** File a judgment lien certificate with the Department of State against both debtors. Record a certified copy in the county of the home and any other property.
- **Rivera Builders.** Likely an empty shell now. Use proceedings supplementary to trace its assets into Rivera Construction Group as a successor or alter ego.
- **Truck.** Transfer to an insider for $1 after suit was filed, while insolvent, with Luis probably still driving it: nearly every badge of fraud. Voidable under § 726.105; recover from the brother in proceedings supplementary.
- **New LLC.** Moving accounts into a new entity is a transfer of the old LLC's assets for no value. Luis's membership interest in the new LLC is subject to a charging order, and as a single-member LLC, foreclosure of the interest under § 605.0503(4) if distributions will not satisfy the judgment.
- **Wife's salary.** If Luis is doing the work and his wife is paid to shield wages, the court can treat the arrangement as a fraudulent transfer of his earnings; expect discovery into who actually works.
- **Home.** If it is their homestead, exempt from the judgment lien regardless of value within acreage limits. If held as tenants by the entireties, it is also beyond Bay Electric's reach because the judgment is against Luis alone, not both spouses, per *Beal Bank*.
- **401(k).** Exempt under § 222.21 and ERISA.
- **Wages.** Nothing to garnish if he draws no salary; if he did, head-of-family status would exempt the first $750 per week.
- **Facts that would change it.** If the wife had also signed the guaranty, the entireties home would be reachable. If the truck was transferred before any dispute arose, for fair value, it stands.

</details>

## Check yourself

1. A creditor has a judgment but never filed a judgment lien certificate. The sheriff levies on the debtor's equipment, and another creditor who filed a certificate last year objects. Who gets paid first?
2. A debtor earns $1,100 per week and supports two children. How much can a creditor garnish?
3. Why does the single-member LLC get weaker protection under § 605.0503 than a multi-member LLC?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The creditor who filed the certificate. Priority runs by filing date under § 55.204, and an unfiled creditor has no lien at all.
2. Nothing, unless the debtor agreed in writing to garnishment. As head of family, the first $750 per week is fully exempt, and amounts above that are exempt absent written consent under § 222.11(2)(b).
3. Because the charging order's purpose is to protect the other members from an unwanted co-owner. When there are no other members, that purpose is absent, so the Legislature allowed foreclosure of the interest when distributions will not satisfy the judgment in a reasonable time.

</details>
