# When you are sued

**Module:** 08 · **Lesson:** 8 · **Time:** ~45 min
**Tags:** `[FL]` `[US]`

## Why this matters to a business owner

The first three weeks after service decide more than the next year. A missed deadline is a default. A late call to the insurer can forfeit a defense already paid for. An email to the plaintiff becomes an admission. A deleted file becomes an adverse inference. This lesson is the sequence of decisions a defendant faces, in order, with the rule behind each.

## The rule

**1. Read the papers and calendar the deadline.** The summons states the response time: 20 days (verify) from service in Florida state court (Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.140(a)), 21 days (verify) in federal court (Fed. R. Civ. P. 12). Service on an LLC is proper on the registered agent, or on a member or manager if the agent is unavailable (§ 48.062).

**2. Notify the insurer immediately.** The insurer's **duty to defend** is broader than its duty to pay: it is triggered if the complaint alleges facts that could fall within coverage, judged from the complaint's four corners against the policy. Late notice is presumed prejudicial to the insurer, and the insured must rebut the presumption to keep coverage (*Bankers Insurance Co. v. Macias*, 475 So. 2d 1216 (Fla. 1985)) (verify). Expect a reservation of rights letter.

**3. Preserve everything.** Issue a written hold. Suspend auto-delete. Do not "clean up" files, social media, or messages (lesson 3).

**4. Do not contact the plaintiff directly if represented.** The ethics rule against contacting a represented party binds lawyers, not clients, so an owner may technically speak to the plaintiff. But anything said is admissible. Route communications through counsel. Do not post about the case.

**5. Decide the forum questions, fast.**

- **Removal to federal court.** If the case could have been filed in federal court, through diversity or a federal question, the defendant may remove within 30 days (verify) of service (28 U.S.C. § 1446(b)). A defendant that is a citizen of Florida cannot remove a diversity case filed in Florida state court (28 U.S.C. § 1441(b)(2)). An LLC's citizenship is that of every member.
- **Arbitration.** If any contract in the relationship has an arbitration clause, move to compel it in the first filing. Litigating first waives it (lesson 5).
- **Venue and personal jurisdiction.** Waived unless raised in the first motion or answer (Rule 1.140(h)).

**6. Motion to dismiss or answer.** A motion to dismiss tests the complaint's legal sufficiency on its face: failure to state a cause of action, missing pre-suit requirements, limitation period apparent on the face, lack of jurisdiction. It tolls the time to answer and rarely ends a case, but it narrows the claims. An answer must admit or deny each paragraph and must plead every **affirmative defense** or lose it: limitations, release, payment, accord and satisfaction, waiver, estoppel, setoff, prior breach.

**7. Counterclaims.** Any claim against the plaintiff arising from the same transaction or occurrence is compulsory (Rule 1.170(a)) and must be filed with the answer or it is barred. Claims against outsiders who owe indemnity are third-party claims (Rule 1.180).

**8. Who is actually sued.** Check whether the complaint names the LLC, the owner personally, or both. The LLC must appear through a lawyer. An owner sued personally may be entitled to indemnification and advancement of defense costs from the LLC under the operating agreement and § 605.0408 (verify). If the complaint pleads piercing facts, the owner's personal assets are in play (lesson 7).

**9. Early settlement tools.** A defendant may serve a proposal for settlement 90 days (verify) after service (lesson 4). A modest early proposal, where the defense is strong, shifts fees from that date.

**10. Budget.** Ask counsel for a phased budget: through the pleadings, through discovery, through summary judgment, through trial. Compare each phase to the realistic exposure, the fee-shifting picture, and the insurance limits.

**Default.** If no paper is filed within the deadline, the clerk enters a default on the plaintiff's request (Rule 1.500(a)). Relief from a default requires a prompt motion showing excusable neglect, a meritorious defense, and due diligence (Rule 1.540(b)).

**Burden.** The plaintiff proves its claim. The defendant proves affirmative defenses, counterclaims, and the elements for vacating a default.

## How courts reason about it

- **Defaults.** Florida policy favors deciding cases on the merits, so courts vacate defaults where the defendant moved quickly and has a real defense. "The registered agent did not forward the papers" has succeeded; "I ignored it" has not.
- **Duty to defend.** Courts compare the complaint to the policy and resolve doubts in favor of a defense. If any count is potentially covered, the insurer defends the whole case. Late notice forfeits coverage only where the insured fails to rebut prejudice.
- **Waiver by litigation conduct.** Arbitration, venue, jurisdiction, and service defenses are lost by participating.
- **Motion to dismiss versus answer.** A defense visible on the face of the complaint, such as limitations or a missed pre-suit notice, can win outright; a fact-dependent defense belongs in an answer and a later summary judgment motion.
- **Personal exposure.** Courts dismiss owners named without piercing allegations or allegations of personal conduct.

## Florida specifics

- Florida follows the majority of states on defaults, affirmative defenses, and compulsory counterclaims, with rules modeled on the federal ones. Its 20-day response period is shorter than most.
- Florida's presumption of prejudice from late notice, rebuttable by the insured, is a middle position among the states (verify).
- The proposal for settlement under § 768.79 is the Florida defendant's distinctive early weapon; it has no equivalent in most states.
- An LLC must be represented by a Florida Bar member in circuit and county court outside small claims. A pro se filing by an owner on the LLC's behalf is a nullity and does not stop a default.

## Angles checklist

- Deadline: date of service, method, on whom, response due when?
- Insurance: every policy that might respond, notice sent in writing, reservation of rights received?
- Hold: issued, to whom, systems covered?
- Forum: removal possible and advisable, arbitration clause, venue clause, personal jurisdiction?
- First filing: motion or answer, and which defenses must be in it?
- Affirmative defenses: which apply on these facts?
- Counterclaims: compulsory ones identified, limitation periods checked?
- Parties: who is named, is the owner properly named, indemnification rights?
- Proposal for settlement: when does the window open, what number?
- Budget: phased cost, realistic range, fee shifting, policy limits.
- Communications: through counsel only.

## Drill

Sunrise Kitchens LLC and its managing member, Tom, are served on April 2 with a Hillsborough circuit court complaint by a former customer alleging breach of contract, FDUTPA, and fraud over a $65,000 kitchen installation. The contract has an arbitration clause and a prevailing-party fee clause. Tom emails the customer that night: "This is ridiculous, the delays were your fault, but I'll give you $5,000 to drop it." He has a general liability policy. He plans to respond himself to save money. Spot the issues.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

- **Deadline.** Response due April 22. Tom's plan to respond himself fails for the LLC, which needs counsel; a pro se filing for the LLC is a nullity and leads to default.
- **Email.** A written statement to a represented plaintiff. The $5,000 offer is likely inadmissible as a settlement offer under § 90.408, but "the delays were your fault" and everything else is an admission against interest and will be an exhibit. Route everything through counsel from now on.
- **Arbitration.** The clause must be invoked in the first filing by motion to compel and stay. Answering or moving to dismiss on the merits risks waiver. The fraud and FDUTPA claims are likely within a broad clause.
- **Insurance.** A general liability policy covers bodily injury and property damage, not breach of contract or fraud, but if the complaint alleges any property damage during installation, the duty to defend may be triggered for the whole case. Give notice now.
- **Tom personally.** Named as managing member. If the complaint alleges only that he signed the contract for the LLC, he should move to dismiss as to himself. If it alleges he personally made fraudulent statements, he stays in for the fraud count.
- **Affirmative defenses and counterclaim.** Customer-caused delay is a defense (prior breach, failure of condition) and supports a compulsory counterclaim for the unpaid balance, if any.
- **Fees.** The contract clause is reciprocal, and FDUTPA's § 501.2105 adds two-way exposure. A proposal for settlement can be served after July 1.
- **Facts that would change it.** If Tom personally and knowingly misstated the cabinet grade, personal fraud liability and possible punitive damages change the exposure; if the clause carves out claims under $75,000, no arbitration.

</details>

## Check yourself

1. A business owner receives a complaint and calls her insurance agent six weeks later, after she has already been defaulted. What problems has she created?
2. The complaint names only the LLC. The owner is confident the plaintiff is wrong. Can the owner file the answer for the LLC to save on legal fees?
3. Why should a defendant with an arbitration clause avoid filing a motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. A default that must be vacated by motion showing excusable neglect, a meritorious defense, and diligence, with no guarantee; and late notice to the insurer, which is presumed prejudicial and may forfeit the defense and coverage unless she can rebut the presumption.
2. No. An LLC must appear through a licensed attorney in Florida circuit and county court outside small claims. An answer signed by the owner is a nullity and the default clock keeps running.
3. Because seeking a merits ruling from the court is conduct inconsistent with the right to arbitrate, and under *Morgan v. Sundance* the other side need not show prejudice to establish waiver. The first filing should be a motion to compel arbitration and stay.

</details>
