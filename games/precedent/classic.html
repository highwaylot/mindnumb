How to play, and why this is a law game

Every round the judge adopts one secret rule about a case. A case is a row of one to four pieces. Each piece has a shape (circle, square, triangle), a color (red, blue, gold), and a size (small or large). Order matters for some rules.

  • Read the docket. You start with a few rulings. The rule is consistent with all of them, and with many other rules you have not thought of yet.
  • File test cases. Build a case and the judge rules on it. Good filings are the ones that split your live hypotheses: change one thing at a time.
  • Predict before you file. Optional, but it is the honest test of whether you understand the rule yet.
  • Sit the bar. Five fresh cases, you call each one. Five for five wins the round. Fewer filings before passing earns a higher rank.
  • Ask the clerk after six filings to learn what kind of thing the rule is about. Read the opinion to give up and see the rule.

This is how courts work. Nobody hands you the rule. You get rulings on facts, you infer the rule, you test it against new facts, and you find out you were wrong when the next case comes out the other way. Passing the bar here means the same thing it means in life: you can predict what the judge will do.
